# Print Design Prompt Engineering Framework

A principle-based system for turning a business brief into one precise image-generation prompt for a print design artwork. Its output answers a specific design problem; it is never a styled version of the business category. The prompt describes the artwork itself and nothing around it: the situation the piece is made for is something the analysis uses, never something the prompt says (Principle 12).

**How to use:** Work through the five phases in Section 2 in order. At the start of each phase, read the matching reference file (Section 3), and open the style bank files that phase calls for (Section 3, loading table). Foundational decisions are explored and locked before the decisions they govern. Imagery is decided per image through `references/08_imagery_roles_and_style_modulation.md`, and the mark through `references/09_brand_mark_identity_and_representation.md`, in every phase (Section 3, loading table). Output the prompt with the template in Section 4 and the rules in Section 5. A brief for several pieces shares Phases 1 and 2, so the pieces read as one family, and repeats Phases 3 to 5 for each medium.

## 1. Principles

1. **Understand before deciding.** The brief states a fraction of what determines good design. Build a working model of the business as its owner and a domain expert would hold it: how the business operates and earns; how the subject is made, delivered, met, and judged; what its advantage means in lived terms; how the customer actually decides and buys; how the category presents itself; what surrounds the piece where it hangs. Understanding is pursued until it yields design-relevant truths: findings that are true, distinctive within the field, perceptible, and relevant to the customer's decision. Every later decision draws on these, not on the brief's words.
2. **Problem before style.** Design starts from a design problem: the strain that appears where the brand's truth, the audience's state, the objective, the medium, and the placement meet. Style, imagery, color, and type are answers to that problem, never starting points. Context dimensions are read through their interactions, because the same attribute means different things to different audiences, placements, and distances. No dimension is interpreted alone.
3. **Insight before concept.** A distinctive idea comes from an insight: a non-obvious truth about this brand, for this reader, at this moment of encounter, that the piece can make felt. The concept is that insight, grounded in the brand's core (what it is to its customer, how it behaves, what territory it can credibly own), made visible as a specific world, device, structure, or behavior, concrete enough to settle later choices. A mood adjective or style label is not a concept.
4. **Associations are hypotheses.** The first association a name, product, category, or place offers is the baseline every competitor shares. Trace it to what it is actually about, keep it only if the brand itself earns it, and express it as this brand's own form of that idea. A fact merely adjacent to the brand constrains fit until the brand's identity connects it to the personality.
5. **Position within the field.** Every category has a grammar of presentation. Conventions that signal belonging (legitimacy, category, price level, trust) are kept and executed in the brand's own way; habits are questioned; clichés that make competitors indistinguishable are replaced. The design is placed in the possibility space on purpose: authentic to its category, its place, and the business as it truly operates, and distinct from its field by idea rather than by volume. Positioning qualities have no fixed visual form: each is read through this audience's codes, price level, and field baseline, so the same quality takes different treatments in different contexts. A design promises something, and the real encounter must be able to keep it.
6. **Range, and difference by role.** A piece is one-dimensional when every layer states the same thing the same way or receives the same treatment. A distinctive piece has one governing concept carried with range: a personality made of qualities held in productive tension, a payoff at each distance from which the piece is actually read, and layers whose attributes derive from their own roles. Coherence comes from a shared logic, not shared values. Tier sets an element's volume, never its voice, and restraint lowers intensity before it removes character.
7. **Approach follows the job.** Each visual layer, and each image within it, receives a kind of making (captured, drawn, constructed, typographic, or a hybrid) according to the kind of truth it must deliver about the subject's reality, and an execution style according to the stance the piece takes toward its subject and viewer. Neither is a default and neither is set by category.
8. **Divided labor.** A piece carries several messages (what the brand is, what it offers, how it should feel, what to do). Each goes to the layer best placed to carry it, once. Layers that already carry a message free the others to carry what they alone can.
9. **Physical reality.** Aspect ratio, physical size, viewing distance, exposure time, substrate, and placement set what can be read and what looks native. Weight comes from scale, color, placement, isolation, and detail together, distributed by reader attention. Canvas area is the last dimension spent. Size is bounded below by the legibility floor and above by tier. Space is a designed layer: content is fitted to the density the medium's scale and viewing conditions support, never the reverse. These conditions govern the design through what they make visible (scale ratios, contrast, density, margins, ground). They are analysis inputs and are not stated in the prompt.
10. **Every finding becomes a consequence.** Analysis exists to change decisions. Each finding is converted into an implication (what it requires, permits, or rules out), each implication into the decisions it informs, and each decision into observable properties and directives. A directive with no chain back to a driver is a default, and a driver that changes no decision was not worth the analysis. Once a finding has become a directive, the finding itself stays in the Decision Context and only its visible effect is written.
11. **Vocabulary follows understanding.** The style bank (`05`, `06`, `07`) widens the candidates a decision can draw on and gives each choice a precise, visible meaning. It is consulted after the drivers exist, never in place of them, and no entry is required, forbidden, or tied to a type of business. A term enters the prompt only if a driver calls for it and it changes something visible.
12. **The prompt depicts the artwork, not its situation.** A generator draws or obeys whatever the prompt says, so every phrase must name something visible inside the canvas or a property of how the canvas is rendered. The situation is outside the canvas: where the piece is placed or mounted, what surrounds it, who views it, from how far and for how long, what it is for, and how and on what it is produced. All of it is analysis input and converts, before writing, into its visible consequence (a scale relationship, a contrast, a margin, a ground character) with the cause left out. Test each phrase by asking whether it can be seen in the image or changes how the image is rendered; if neither, remove it. Narrating the piece as an object or an event (what it is made to do, where it is meant to hang, how it is viewed) is situation language even when it is phrased in visual words. The medium appears once, as the format name that tells what kind of canvas it is. Production method and material appear only as the visible character of the making (how paint, ink, paper, or cut layers look on the surface), never as manufacturing facts.
13. **Images have roles; styles have an identity.** Every image is briefed as its own instance by its job, grade, how its own content is read, its relations to type, siblings, and ground, and the kind of truth it must carry. One style identity (a signature of primary anchors) governs the piece, and each image expresses it at the register its role calls for, derived through explicit dials. Role changes how much of each trait shows, never which style it is. Identity moves to the dials a role can afford and is never deleted by a generic fade, blur, or opacity reduction. No image inherits the hero's treatment, no image is described by a role word in the prompt, and the images are judged together as one composition.

14. **The mark is an identity asset, not styled text.** The primary mark is designed as one coherent unit with its own silhouette, structure, built lettering, and symbol or boundary where the drivers call for them, and it must pass tests that separate it from a headline (blur, headline, and swap). Construction (open, implied boundary, explicit enclosure, symbol-led) is decided across candidates that differ in idea, with no default in either direction, and each construction qualifies only through specificity. The master mark's identity is constant; its role (dominant identity, supporting element, discreet signature), variant, scale, placement, and detail adapt to each composition's hierarchy and never grant it area by default. The brand name appears once, in the mark.

Every list, ratio, or reference in this skill is a tendency along a continuum; where a brief falls outside it, extend the principle. Hard-content fidelity, the output template, and the execution rules are fixed.

## 2. Master Decision Flow

Each phase takes the accumulated Decision Context, does ordered work, and produces entries that later phases depend on.

```
Phase 1 Understanding  →  Phase 2 Concept & Identity  →  Phase 3 Composition  →  Phase 4 Prompt Synthesis  →  Phase 5 Verification
```

* **Phase 1: Understanding & Design Problem** (`references/01_strategic_analysis_and_deconstruction.md`)
  * *Takes:* the brief.
  * *Does:* (1) Run each assessment in the register as question, finding, implication, decision informed, covering the stated brief and then the subject beyond it, including the recognition and mark field (`09`, Section 2). (2) Tier content, set the primary focus, derive viewing conditions, legibility floors, and capacity, and set the reception balance and a provisional pictorial ceiling. (3) Inventory the jobs images could do, admit and grade each instance, set the image economy, and open the image role ledger (`08`, Section 1). (4) Weigh the drivers, read their interactions, name the tensions, and state the design problem, insight, and brand core. (5) Compile the implication map.
  * *Consults (after its own findings exist, as a completeness check only):* style bank `05` Section 1, then `06` Facets K, L, M, N, A5a, A5b, P1.
  * *Produces:* the subject model, tiers and focus, floors and capacity, the mark's job and distinction requirement, problem, insight, brand core, implication map, and the image role ledger (job, grade, subject, and driver for every image instance), each finding tagged stated, inferred, or unknown.
* **Phase 2: Concept & Identity** (`references/02_visual_identity_and_style_synthesis.md`)
  * *Takes:* the implication map, brand core, insight, tiers, and viewing conditions.
  * *Does:* (1) Position the piece within the category field, derive the concept, personality, and mood, and allocate each message to its carrier. (2) Brief each visual layer and each image role from its job and the subject's truth, complete its role profile (`08`, Section 2), assign its kind of making, set the piece's one style signature so that it can host every admitted role (`08`, Section 3), derive each image's register through the dials (`08`, Section 4), and confirm or revise the pictorial commitment. (3) Choose movement, form language, color logic, typographic voice, ornament, and design the master mark across construction candidates with its reduction ladder (`09`, Section 3). (4) Check all of it as one system and lock it.
  * *Consults:* style bank `05` in full, `08` Sections 2 to 4, `09` Sections 1 to 4, then the `06` facets each decision needs (A to C, E to J, O, I3 to I7 for the mark, and P2 and P3 for signature, family, and reach), widening candidates by `05` Section 2.
  * *Produces:* locked identity decisions: field position, concept, personality, and mood as visible behavior; message allocation; per-layer kind of making and stance; the style signature; each image's role profile, kind of making, and register; pictorial grades; movement and form language; palette roles, logic, hues, and proportions; display and supporting typographic voices; ornament; and the master mark (construction, boundary decision, lettering, symbol, color logic, reduction ladder).
* **Phase 3: Composition** (`references/03_compositional_architecture_and_spatial_systems.md`)
  * *Takes:* the locked identity, tiers, focus, and capacity.
  * *Does:* (1) Confirm tiering and focus against the identity (a change returns through the conflict rule). (2) Set the density target and lock a composition strategy that carries the concept through structure. (3) Brief every element (job, tier, distance, relationships) and derive its zone, position, size, presence, and treatment. (4) Derive each image's placement and its relations to type, siblings, and ground, including the designed calm regions of any image that hosts type (`08`, Section 5). (5) Derive the mark's role, variant, scale, clear space, and placement from the hierarchy (`09`, Section 5). (6) Audit the weight (images, grounds, and the mark included), expression, and range the attributes actually produce.
  * *Consults:* `06` Facets D, G, H, N (and L, M for the medium), by `05` Section 1; `08` Section 5; `09` Section 5; `06` Facets P1 and I7.
  * *Produces:* a locked composition and, for every element and image, its zone, position, relative size, weight and color relationship, typographic treatment, rendering, register, and presence, each traceable.
* **Phase 4: Prompt Synthesis** (`references/04_prompt_synthesis_and_output_execution.md`)
  * *Takes:* every locked decision and attribute.
  * *Does:* (1) Fill the template slot by slot from macro concept to zonal implementation, converting each decision into observable properties and relationships, with one entry per image written as its register (`08`, Section 6) and the mark written as one constructed unit (`09`, Section 6). (2) Sweep every phrase against the frame boundary (Principle 12): convert what describes the situation into its visible consequence, and delete what has none.
  * *Consults:* `07` in full (Sections 7 and 8 for image registers and the mark); `08` Section 6; `09` Section 6; `06` Facets L, M to check each decision against the medium.
  * *Produces:* the prompt, with no attribute left to default.
* **Phase 5: Verification** (`references/04`, Section 5)
  * *Takes:* the prompt and the Decision Context.
  * *Does:* Run the checklist, trace each failure to the decision that caused it, revise there, and re-derive what it governs.
  * *Consults:* `07` Sections 5 to 8, `08` Section 7, `09` Section 7, `05` Section 3, `06` Facets M and N5.
  * *Produces:* the final prompt.

### Decision Context & Decision Protocol

**Decision Context:** Compiled in Phase 1, extended in every later phase, consulted by every decision. It is also a ledger: each committed decision is entered with the drivers behind it and the information status of those drivers, each new decision is checked against all entries, and revising an entry reopens everything derived from it. It holds: business and positioning; objective, reader task, and intended response; audience and moment of encounter; medium and physical scale; placement and register; the subject model (design-relevant truths: operations, reality, lived USP, customer decision and buying behavior, category field, surroundings); content load, tiers, and primary focus; parameter weights, interactions, and tensions; the design problem, insight, and brand core; the implication map linking each weighted driver to the decisions it informs; the image role ledger (one entry per image instance: job, grade, role profile, kind of making, register, drivers); then, as decisions are committed, field position, concept, personality, mood, approach per layer, style signature, and style direction.

**Foundational decisions** govern all others: primary focus, message allocation, concept and personality, mood, kind of making and execution style per layer, the style signature and the set of image roles with their grades (the pictorial commitment), movement, palette architecture, typographic voice, mark construction and role, and composition strategy (including its density target). Each follows these steps, with divergence widest for the decisions that govern the most:
1. **Frame:** State the job of the decision and which drivers (weighted findings, interactions, and tensions in the implication map) bear on it, and what each implies for it.
2. **Diverge:** Map the possibility space for the decision (the axes along which answers can genuinely vary, including the variables on which this business differs from its field), then generate candidates that sit apart on those axes and differ in underlying idea, each built from a different dominant context dimension or interaction and stated as concrete visible behavior so it can be judged. Include three depths: the apparent (what proximity, category grammar, or the prototype suggests), the underlying (what that is about), and the personal (what this brand's character would choose). The first coherent candidate is usually the nearest one, so the search continues past it. The apparent is the baseline to beat and is chosen only when it is the strongest fit. For decisions of style, medium, type, color, ornament, mark, and composition, the candidates are widened with the style bank following `05`, Section 2 (at least three families, one from outside the category's usual sources).
3. **Test:**
   * *Traceability:* it follows from named drivers and their implications.
   * *Tailoring:* would it change if the business, audience, medium, or placement changed? If not, it is generic.
   * *Earned connection:* every context fact it draws on is connected to the brand's own identity, claim, or audience, not merely nearby.
   * *Resolution:* it resolves a tension of this brief.
   * *Range:* it carries something other layers do not, and it holds a tension rather than one note.
   * *Belonging and distinction:* it keeps the field's signals of belonging so it looks native in its category and placement, and differs from its neighbors by idea, not by volume.
   * *Function:* it is legible at the medium's distances and settles downstream choices.
   * *Credibility:* the signals it sends match what the business is and delivers, at the scale and means it operates with, so the customer's real encounter confirms them.
   * *Coherence:* it agrees with the foundational decisions already made.
4. **Specify:** Resolve it to a level that discriminates (`02`, Section 5; the vocabulary and conversions in `06` and `07`, Section 6).
5. **Lock:** Check the foundational decisions against each other as one system. Lock them in two stages: identity decisions at the end of Phase 2, composition strategy at the start of Phase 3, checked against the identity. No derived decision precedes the lock that governs it.

**Derived decisions** are every attribute of every element and layer, including each image's register, which is derived from its role profile and the signature, never inherited from another image. Each is derived from the element's tier (when it is read), its purpose (what it does for the reader), its presence (whether and how visibly it exists), the physical conditions under which it is seen, and the foundational decisions it serves. Test each for necessity, proportionate weight as its attributes actually produce it, voice, legibility at the floor, and consistency with siblings. Commit each as an explicit directive.

**Traceability chain:** Every directive traces from driver to implication to foundational decision to derived attribute to directive. What governs more is explored more widely and locked first; what is governed is derived, not reinvented. A directive with no chain is a default, and a weighted driver that reaches no decision signals analysis left unfinished.

**Conflict rule:** When a derived decision cannot satisfy its governing decisions without breaking hierarchy or legibility, trace the conflict to its cause in the ledger, revise the foundational decision, and re-derive everything entered after it that depends on it. Never patch downstream.

**Working discipline:** Deliberation is internal. Each foundational decision is explored separately before any prompt text is drafted, and only committed directives appear in the prompt.

## 4. Universal Output Template

The final deliverable is ALWAYS a single, self-contained, semantically dense prompt in this structure:

```
[Aspect Ratio] [Format Name] for "[Brand Name]".
[Creative Concept & Personality: the single idea as direct visual direction only (what is visibly present, how forms, type, and color behave, and the atmosphere that results), with no rationale]. [Governing Design Movement / Tradition resolved to its distinguishing traits, with the supporting voice when hybrid]. [Color Palette Architecture: the color roles, the hues chosen for them, and their relative proportions]. [Form Language, Edge Behavior, and Overall Aesthetic Tone].

PRIMARY LOGO MARK & BRANDING:
[The mark as one constructed unit, described by construction and never as a bare typeface or the word "logo": its type and how its parts are held together; its silhouette; the lettering's structural traits and the moves built into it (the brand name appears here, verbatim, in quotation marks, once); the symbol as simplified visible form, if any; its boundary (a named enclosure with its form and the lettering's relation to it, an implied boundary, or an open contour stated), with the concept as visible form in one of these; its own color logic and separation from the ground; its internal levels and descriptor; its variant; and its relative scale and clear space in the hierarchy. The mark remains structurally independent of background elements].

COMPOSITION & LAYOUT ZONING:
[Zonal structure organized along the canvas's dominant axis by content group. The density target and negative-space allocation (outer margins, separations between groups, regions left deliberately empty). The display and supporting typographic voices per hierarchy level, resolved to structural traits. Position on the canvas and relative scale, expressed as relationships, for the focal anchor, each image (with the calm regions where type sits over an image, by position and extent), tagline, information blocks, and action badges. Every hard-content string appears verbatim, in quotation marks, at its position].

VISUAL ELEMENTS & RENDERING TECHNIQUE:
[One entry per image admitted, or a statement that none is used. Each entry: its subject (supplied subjects only), complexity, and relative scale; its kind of making; its execution style resolved to observable properties (vantage, light, depth, subject state, and grade for captured work; lineage, medium, line, and simplification for drawn work; geometric logic and rhythm for constructed work); and its register, the visible settings of detail, tonal range, color breadth, edge, depth and focus, crop, mark and surface visibility, and specificity that distinguish it from the piece's other images, with its edge and crop behavior. Then which image or layer leads and the shared properties (palette logic, edge character, light, surface behavior) that keep every image one style identity, including when kinds of making differ].

BACKGROUND, BORDERS & FINISH:
[Ground character and visible surface treatment (including any image, texture, or pattern field that forms the ground, at its register, with its calm regions by position and value band), borders, decorative motifs, and edge treatments, each carrying the concept's character at its stated visibility, or a statement that a layer is deliberately plain].

TECHNICAL & EXECUTION CONSTRAINTS:
[Rendering rules for the image: layout structure, contrast behavior, photographic or non-photographic stance, execution quality, and legibility expressed as relative scale and contrast between hierarchy levels. Imagery is not restated here].
```

**Slot discipline.** The opening sentence identifies the piece and holds only the aspect ratio, the format name (what kind of canvas it is), and the brand name. It carries no placement, setting, audience, purpose, substrate, production method, or description of the design; those belong to the slots after it, and only where they are visible in the image. Every slot below it holds depiction and rendering only (Principle 12).

## 5. Execution Rules & Prompt Directives

1. **Output Raw Prompt Only:** Output only the single prompt block unless asked otherwise, except when information that cannot be inferred and would change a foundational decision or the hard content is missing; ask for it concisely first. The Decision Context, protocol work, and audits stay internal and are completed before the prompt is written.
2. **Never Invent Contents:** Enforce all hard content (copy, numbers, addresses, menu items, prices, and any other required element) exactly as supplied. Never omit required text or add items.
3. **No Meta-Commentary, No Situation:** Every phrase is an objective, observable visual directive about something inside the canvas or how the canvas is rendered. Reasoning, justification, intent, evaluative language, and framework terminology never appear, and neither does the situation: where the piece is placed, mounted, or surrounded, who views it and from how far, what it is for, and how or on what it is produced. Each such fact is converted into its visible consequence before writing (Principle 12).
4. **Preserve Generative Freedom:** Specify structure, relationships, and aesthetic parameters. Leave fine line rendering, lighting falloff, and organic texture to the model.
5. **English Output:** Write the entire prompt in English whatever language the brief uses. Only literal hard content is reproduced verbatim in its original language, in quotation marks.
6. **Relational Direction:** Direct position, scale, and proportion through relationships (order, alignment, relative size, weight) and describe color by hue and role. Measurements, coordinates, angles, counts, and color codes appear only when the brief supplies them (the aspect ratio is derived from supplied dimensions).
7. **Economy:** State each decision once, in the slot where it belongs.
8. **Specificity:** Every stylistic term is resolved to a level that discriminates within its category. A category name never stands alone as a decision.
9. **Bank vocabulary in, bank apparatus out:** Names of lineages, techniques, materials, and typographic structures appear with their distinguishing traits. Facet letters, entry codes, and bank terminology never appear. Living artists, brands, characters, and other protected sources are never named as a style. The national flag, state emblem, sacred text, and regulated marks are never decorative content (`05`, Section 3).
10. **Reliability never trumps fidelity:** A generator's weakness with an element (long copy, prices, small print) changes how the element is directed, never whether required content appears (`07`, Section 5). QR codes, regulated marks, and supplied logos are reserved as clean areas or directed by canvas position, never recreated.
11. **Images by role, in visible terms:** Each image is directed as its own entry with its own register, derived from its role profile and the piece's style signature (`08`). Role words (hero, background, decorative, supporting, to host the text), purposes, and situations never appear; their visible consequences do (a calm region by position and value band, a shared framing rule for a series, lower tonal range and softer edges than the leading image). An image depicts only supplied content. Every image keeps the signature's primary anchors, and no image's treatment is copied from another's.
12. **The mark is a unit, stated once:** The primary mark is directed as one constructed entry in its slot (`09`, Section 6; `07`, Section 8), never as the name in a typeface. Construction is chosen across open, implied-boundary, enclosed, and symbol-led candidates and qualified by the tests in `09`; no construction is a default. Its prominence enters as scale ratio, clear space, and contrast, derived from the hierarchy, and no role word appears. The brand name string appears in the mark only, unless the brief places it again.
