# Compositional Architecture & Spatial Systems

*Used in Phase 3 of `SKILL.md`. Every decision follows the Decision Protocol in `SKILL.md`, Section 2.*

Composition is not a container for the concept; it is one of its carriers. Phase 3 confirms the tiering and primary focus against the locked identity decisions (a change returns through the conflict rule), locks a composition strategy that expresses the concept, then derives every element's zone, position, size, and presence from tier, purpose, and the foundational decisions.

---

## 1. Composition Strategy (Foundational)

Decide, as one locked decision: the axis commitment, the focal anchor, the reading path, the density and negative-space stance, and the structural behavior (how rhythm, tension, symmetry or asymmetry, and edge relationships express the concept's personality). Diverge across candidates that differ in organizing idea, test them as in `SKILL.md`, and lock. A composition that would suit any brand on this canvas is generic.

### Zoning
1. **Read the canvas:** dominant axis and visibility map (`01`).
2. **Form zones from content groups:** the number of zones is the number of groups the brief has, not a fixed count.
3. **Size zones by tier:** area follows attention weight, and lower-tier zones stay compact or merge.
4. **Set the path:** the reading path is a consequence of weight gradients and directional cues, designed for the canvas and its anchor.
* **Axis tendencies** are on a continuum. Horizontal canvases flow along the long axis with the entry anchor at the start. Vertical canvases stack content in path order with the anchor in the best-visibility region. Near-equal axes use central or radial anchors positioned to stay visible behind people and objects. Extreme ratios commit to the axis and concentrate weight where visibility is reliable.

## 2. Typographic Hierarchy

* **Scale contrast:** The primary headline holds at least a 2:1 scale jump over the next level, and every level steps down clearly. Ratios are minimums that set relationships, never size targets. The ratio to body copy widens as the medium grows, because headlines scale with distance and body copy does not.
* **Sizing:** When a text element is the primary focus it is sized for the farthest distance at which Tier 1 is read; otherwise the top text level is sized by its own tier. Body copy is sized from its floor. Prices and highlights scale independently by tier.
* **Voices:** The display voice carries identity and the supporting voice carries information (`02`, Section 4). Weight, size, and spacing express hierarchy within each voice's character.

---

## 3. Grouping & Devices

* **Group by relationship:** proximity, similarity, alignment, continuity, and common region. Enclosure is one cue and the heaviest.
* **Lightest sufficient device:** Use the weakest cue that achieves the grouping or separation, because every device adds weight the tier budget must pay for. Absence of any boundary is a valid choice.
* **Form follows concept:** Any device that exists (container, badge, label, rule, divider) takes its shape, corners, edges, and line quality from the movement's form language and the concept. A stock-shaped container is a default. A device around a single item is ornament and must pass the ornament test (`02`, Section 6).
* **Device economy:** One device language, with as few distinct device types as the hierarchy requires.

---

## 4. Negative Space

* **Macro:** Maintain an outer margin proportional to the canvas that protects against trim, mounting, and folds. Never crowd text or the mark against the edges.
* **Micro:** Maintain internal padding within groups to prevent compression.
* **Allocation:** Negative space is continuous. It rises with quieter positioning, longer reading time, and surplus canvas relative to floor-sized content, and it falls with fast-read, value-driven, high-density contexts and small canvases where the floor binds. It is also a compositional voice that can carry the concept.

## 5. Attention Balance & Sizing

### 5.1 Balance by Attention
* **Tier 1** forms the dominant anchor. **Tier 2** is distributed to counterweight the anchor and carry the eye along the path. **Tier 3** stays compact and quiet, as one group or several.
* **Distribute weight:** Do not cluster content into one zone and leave others inert. Elements related by meaning stay together; elements merely of the same kind need not.
* **Weight across dimensions:** Hierarchy holds across every weight dimension (`01`, Section 4) together. A small element that shares a higher tier's color relationship or occupies a prime position still competes.
* **Area is spent last:** Build hierarchy first with contrast, isolation, placement, and color. No element is owed area by its category or role.
* **Weight audit:** After attributes are set, assess the weight each element actually produces, including grounds, bands, borders, and fields (which carry weight by area alone), and compare it to tier order. Reduce or remove anything that outweighs its tier. A hierarchy declared in words does not replace one the attributes produce. Ornament stays recessive in color, scale, and placement.
* **Expression audit:** Check for bareness. A layer sitting at default without the concept calling for stillness is given the concept's character at the volume its tier allows. Remove only what adds weight without meaning.
* **Range audit:** Check that the piece pays off at each distance from which it is read (`02`, Section 1) and does not repeat one note across layers.

### 5.2 Scale-Aware Sizing
Size is a band: bounded below by the legibility floor at the distance the tier is actually read, bounded above by tier. Choose within it by hierarchy and balance. On large media, lower tiers sit near their floors, headroom goes to Tier 1 and negative space, and a crowded large canvas signals sizing from canvas proportion instead of from the floor. On small media, every element stays readable at its floor, count is reduced, and content is grouped.

### 5.3 Placement Logic
Every element's position, extent, and grouping is derived from:
* **Function:** where the reader needs it and where the viewer physically stands when they do.
* **Relationship:** nearness to what it explains, qualifies, or completes.
* **Counterweight:** its contribution to balance around the anchor.
* **Form of the group:** compact or spread, single or several, how much of an edge or axis it spans (spanning a whole edge needs justification), standing free or integrated.
* **Overlap:** Elements never overlap or intrude on text. Overlap among non-text elements is deliberate integration.

### 5.4 Pictorial Subject: Role, Scale, Placement
* **Role:** primary anchor, supporting element, or ornament, as ranked by tier. There is one primary anchor, and a pictorial subject holds that role only when it is the Tier 1 element serving the objective.
* **Scale:** By sufficiency (`01`, Section 4). Registration at distance is tried through contrast, isolation, and placement before area. As information load rises, the subject recedes in scale, then visibility, toward ambient or absent. Surplus canvas goes to anchor scale and negative space, not to the subject by default.
* **Placement:** By function, relationship to what it depicts, and counterweight. Supporting or ornamental subjects take recessive color relationships and positions.

### 5.5 Purpose, Presence & Visibility
* **Purpose:** to be understood (clarity first, voice second), to attract or persuade (the concept at full strength for its tier), to organize, or to shape atmosphere (reduced weight, competing with nothing).
* **Presence is a continuum** from absent through barely perceptible and supporting to prominent. Purpose and tier set it at the lowest visibility where the element still does its job.
* **Element brief:** Before setting an element's attributes, state its job for the reader, tier, reading distance, and relationship to its neighbors. Derive size, position, color, type voice, rendering, finish, and complexity from that brief and the foundational decisions it serves. An attribute that cannot be traced is a default, and identical attributes across elements with different briefs show a skipped derivation.
* **Sibling consistency:** Elements of the same tier and purpose share a treatment, and elements that differ in purpose differ by exactly that difference.

### 5.6 No Category Defaults
Treatments, positions, and dominance are never attached to element categories or repeated across briefs by habit. No canvas edge or end of the path is reserved for a content category, and no category, including the mark, is dominant by default: dominance goes to the primary focus.
