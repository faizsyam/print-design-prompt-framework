# Brand Mark: Identity & Representation

*Used in Phases 1 to 5 of `SKILL.md` (Section 3 lists which sections each phase opens). It holds the full method for the foundational decision "mark construction and role"; `02`, Section 7 states how it joins the identity decisions. Vocabulary lives in `06`, Facet I. Prompt phrasing lives in `07`, Section 8. Every decision here follows the Decision Protocol in `SKILL.md`, Section 2, and is entered in the ledger.*

A brand's name set in a display typeface is a headline. A mark is a designed identity asset: one coherent unit with its own structure, silhouette, and character, made to be recognized, recalled, and reproduced, and built so that it stands apart from the typography around it. Treating the mark as styled text is the commonest cause of logos that cannot be told from the headline. This module separates two jobs that were previously merged: **designing the mark** (a durable identity, decided once per brand from the brand's own drivers) and **representing the mark** (how that identity appears in one composition, decided per piece from its objective, hierarchy, and medium). Identity stays constant; prominence, scale, placement, and variant adapt.

---

## 0. Working Principles

1. **The mark is an asset, not a layer of typography.** It is designed as a unit. Setting the name in the display voice is not a design of the mark.
2. **Distinction is earned by construction.** A mark separates from ordinary text through structure, silhouette, lettering built for this brand, symbol, boundary, or a combination. Size, weight, and color alone do not make a mark.
3. **Construction is a decision across candidates, never a default.** Open, contained, symbol-led, and combined constructions are all evaluated against the drivers. Enclosure is neither required nor avoided, and a mark in any construction must pass the same distinction tests.
4. **Identity is constant; representation adapts.** The master mark's structure, silhouette family, lettering character, symbol, and color logic persist. Its role, scale, variant, placement, contrast, and detail level follow the composition.
5. **Role follows the communication hierarchy.** The mark may be the dominant identity element, a supporting element, or a discreet signature. The objective and tiers decide, not the category of element (`03`, Section 5.6).
6. **The mark is a carrier of the concept, concentrated and specific.** It is the most concentrated statement of the concept (`02`, Section 1) through form, not through pasted motif or product depiction.
7. **A mark is only as good as its reduction.** It is designed together with the simplified forms it must take at small scale, in one color, and on different grounds.

## 1. What Makes a Mark a Mark

A mark has five properties. Weakness in several is the signature of styled text.

| Property | Meaning | Visible evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Unity** | reads as one object, not as words on a line | a definite overall silhouette; parts held by shared edges, alignment, shape, or a boundary |
| **Ownability** | specific to this brand and not transferable to another | lettering moves, a symbol, a boundary form, or a structure derived from this name and brand core |
| **Fixity** | its form is constant and is not reflowed like text | defined proportions between parts; a fixed lockup |
| **Separation** | perceptibly distinct from adjacent content and from the ground | a boundary, an implied block, an isolating clear space plus a distinct silhouette, or a color field of its own |
| **Reduction** | survives one color, small size, distance, and different grounds | few parts, strokes sized to a floor, a simplified variant |

### Tests

Run these on every candidate and on the final mark (internally; none appears in the prompt).

* **Blur test:** blur the letters. A distinct silhouette or structure must remain.
* **Headline test:** set the mark beside the display headline. They must differ in structure (construction, silhouette, lettering built for the mark, containment), not only in size, weight, or color. If swapping the mark's lettering for the display voice changes little, the mark is a headline.
* **Swap test:** replace the brand name with another. If the construction still looks like a stock layout, it is a template and not this brand's mark.
* **Reduction test:** reduce to one color and to the smallest size the mark must take; it must remain one recognizable unit.
* **Ground test:** place it on the lightest, darkest, and busiest grounds in the piece; it must keep its separation.

## 2. The Mark's Job and Distinction Requirement (Phases 1 and 2)

### 2.1 What to establish in Phase 1

Run these as assessments with the usual chain (`01`, Section 0). They set how much work the mark must do.

* **Recognition already supplied:** whether the brand is established, whether a mark exists and is supplied (Section 4), where else the audience meets the brand and its mark, and how familiar the name is.
* **Recognition the piece must build:** whether identifying the brand is the Tier 1 job, a supporting job, or a quiet signature (sets the role, Section 5).
* **The name as raw material:** length and word count, letter shapes, presence of initials or an acronym, a place, person, or number in it, its meaning and sound, script and reading direction (`01`, Section 1).
* **The field's mark grammar:** how competitors' marks are constructed (open wordmarks, badges, symbols, characters), which conventions signal legitimacy, trust, or category (signals of belonging), which are habits, and which are clichés (`01`, Section 2).
* **Application range:** the media, sizes, grounds, and making methods the mark must live on (Facet I2, Facets L and M). These set reduction needs and are analysis input; they are not stated in the prompt.
* **Means:** who maintains and reproduces the mark, and what finish they can sustain.

### 2.2 Distinction requirement

The **distinction requirement** is how much construction the mark needs to be recognized and separated. It is high when recognition must be built from nothing, the mark is read from far, grounds are busy, competitors' marks cluster close to this name's obvious treatment, or the mark is the Tier 1 element. It is lower when recognition is already supplied by the setting, the mark is small and surrounded by calm ground, or the name itself is short, unusual, and distinctive in shape. The requirement sets how many distinction levers (Section 3.2) must work and how strongly. It never reaches zero: even a discreet signature must pass the headline test.

## 3. Designing the Master Mark (Phase 2)

### 3.1 Anatomy

A mark is assembled from these components. Any may be absent if the mark remains a unit and passes the tests.

* **Name treatment:** how the name is built into the mark (the moves in Facet I5), as lettering made for this brand rather than a typeface chosen for headlines.
* **Symbol:** a pictorial, abstract, monogram, or character element, derived from the name, the subject's world, the place, the founder, or the brand core (Facet I6).
* **Boundary:** an explicit enclosure, an implied boundary, or an open silhouette (3.3).
* **Structure:** how the parts relate in scale, position, and alignment: stacked, horizontal, circular, tiered, interlocked, nested, or modular (Facet I1).
* **Color logic of the mark:** its own fills and strokes, number of colors, relation to the ground, and a one-color form.
* **Internal levels:** the parts and their order within the mark. A wordmark may be dominant, a symbol may lead, or the two may be equal; this follows the construction. A category descriptor is clean, highly legible, and subordinate within the mark. The tagline is a separate supporting element placed in Phase 3.

### 3.2 Distinction levers

Distinction comes from one or more of these levers (details in Facet I3). Choose the levers the requirement calls for, and ground each in the name, the brand core, or the concept.

| Lever | Creates distinction by |
| :--- | :--- |
| Silhouette | a definite, memorable outer contour that survives blur |
| Structure | a specific arrangement or interlock of parts that a template would not produce |
| Lettering | letterforms altered or built for the mark (ligatures, shared strokes, custom terminals, substituted letters, constructed counters) |
| Symbol | an idea of this brand made form |
| Boundary | an enclosure or implied boundary whose form, proportion, and relation to the lettering are specific |
| Color and material identity | a color relationship or material construction that belongs to the mark |

One lever at high strength or several at moderate strength can meet the requirement. A boundary alone, around lettering set as a headline, is one weak lever; so is a decorative symbol beside a plain headline.

### 3.3 The boundary decision

Boundary is decided across candidates, with no preferred outcome. The constructions to consider (Facet I4):

* **Explicit enclosure:** the mark sits inside a defined form (roundel, shield, arch, label, pill, ticket, seal, window, ribbon, geometric polygon, or a form from the subject's world).
* **Implied boundary:** a block is created without an outline (a shaped color field, paired bars, bracket devices, a negative-space cut, lettering stacked to a defined block contour).
* **Open construction:** no boundary; distinction rests on silhouette, structure, lettering, and symbol.
* **Symbol-led or combined:** the symbol carries the unit, with the name locked to it by structure.

Evaluate each against the drivers:

| Criterion | Asks |
| :--- | :--- |
| Recognition job and distinction requirement | which construction gives the recognition the piece must build, at the scale the mark is read |
| Name properties | whether the name is short or long, one word or several, tall or wide, containing a letter or number that can be built on |
| Field grammar | which constructions the field's marks use, so that the choice keeps a signal of belonging and still separates from neighbors |
| Grounds | whether grounds are busy or calm, light or dark, textured or flat, so the mark keeps separation on them |
| Reduction | whether the construction holds in one color and at the smallest size |
| Application and making | what the marks' media and means can hold (analysis input) |
| Concept | whether the boundary or its absence carries the idea (a boundary can be the concept's form) |
| Hierarchy role | how the construction behaves when it is dominant, supporting, or a small signature |
| Typographic voices | whether the mark's lettering and the display voice can differ in structure |
| Producibility | whether a generator can render the construction reliably (`07`, Section 5) |

**Qualification of each construction.**
* **An enclosed mark** qualifies only if the boundary's form, proportion, and relation to the lettering are specific to this brand (how the lettering sits inside: margin, alignment, touching or breaking the boundary, shaped by it) and the lettering is built, not merely set. A stock badge around a typeface name is as indistinct as plain text.
* **An open mark** qualifies only if it passes the blur, headline, and swap tests through silhouette, structure, lettering, or symbol. An open wordmark that differs from the headline by size and color alone fails.
* **An implied boundary** qualifies if the block it creates is definite and constant across variants.

Enclosure is chosen when it is the strongest candidate against the criteria; open construction is chosen when it is. Record the rejected candidates with a one-line reason tied to a driver.

### 3.4 Relation to the typographic voices

The mark's lettering and the piece's display voice (`02`, Section 4) relate in one of three ways, decided from the concept: **derived** (the display voice shares the family or structural traits of the mark's lettering at headline level, while the mark remains a construction of its own), **contrasting** (the two differ in structure on purpose), or **neutral** (the display voice stays out of the mark's way). In every case the mark's lettering differs from the display voice by at least one structural property (construction, customization, containment, or silhouette). The supporting voice keeps its own job (`02`, Section 4).

### 3.5 Concept in the mark

The concept appears as visible form in at least one lever: a symbol that embodies it, a lettering move that carries it, a boundary shaped by it, a structure that enacts it. Where heritage, regional, or sacred sources are used, they enter as structure or idea and follow `05`, Section 3. The mark never depends on product imagery, background, or seasonal props to be recognized (Section 3.7).

### 3.6 Process (Frame, Diverge, Test, Specify, Lock)

1. **Frame:** state the mark's job, the distinction requirement, and the application range (Section 2).
2. **Diverge:** generate candidates that differ in construction idea, not only in typeface or enclosure shape: at least one open construction, one with an explicit or implied boundary, and one led by a symbol or a structural move, unless the brief fixes the construction (supplied mark, Section 4) or a driver rules one out, in which case state the driver. Include the three depths: the apparent (the name in a display face, with or without a stock badge), the underlying (what the name and the brand are about), and the personal (what this brand's character would build). Widen with Facets I3 to I6 and, for lineages, A and O (`05`, Section 2).
3. **Test:** the decision-protocol tests (`SKILL.md`, Section 2) and the mark tests in Section 1, with the boundary criteria in 3.3.
4. **Specify:** resolve the chosen candidate to the anatomy in 3.1, to the level that discriminates (`02`, Section 5).
5. **Reduction ladder:** define the master mark and the variants it will take (Section 5.3), stating what stays constant and what simplifies at each step.
6. **Lock** with the other identity decisions (`SKILL.md`, Section 2). The master mark's structure is locked at the end of Phase 2; its role and variant are derived in Phase 3.

### 3.7 Independence

The mark is structurally independent of background, imagery, ornament, and the surrounding typography (`02`, Section 2.5). It represents the institution, not one product. No other layer carries its recognition, and it carries none of theirs.

## 4. Supplied Marks

When the brief supplies a mark, it is a fixed asset (`01`, Section 3): it is not redesigned, restyled, or recreated. The decisions that remain are its role, variant (only as supplied), scale, clear space, contrast against the ground, and placement (Section 5). Reserve its area as directed in `07`, Section 5. When the brief supplies only a name, the mark is designed as in Section 3. When the brief supplies a mark whose character conflicts with the piece's identity, the piece's identity yields: palette, type, and ornament are adjusted to host it.

## 5. Role & Representation (Phase 3)

### 5.1 Role

Role is a tier decision made from the objective, reader task, recognition already supplied, mode of engagement, and the movement's weight distribution (`01`, Section 3; `03`, Section 5.6). It is confirmed in Phase 3 against the composition.

| Role | When | Tendencies (derived, not fixed) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dominant identity** | recognition is the Tier 1 job (a name-first piece, a new brand, a fascia that must identify) | the mark is the focal anchor; scale from the top of its band; its distinction levers fully visible; space around it to isolate it; the headline-test difference is most important here because the name is also the main text |
| **Supporting element** | identity is Tier 2: the offer or subject leads while the brand must still register | moderate scale; detail resolved to the variant that holds at that scale; separation from the ground retained; placed in the path of the eye after the anchor |
| **Discreet signature** | the brand is already known by the setting, or the message leads and the brand closes it | compact variant near its floor; unit and silhouette intact; low contrast competition with the message; placed where the path ends or the group closes |

The mark's role never grants it area. Area is the last dimension spent (`01`, Section 3). A dominant mark gains weight through isolation, contrast, and silhouette first.

### 5.2 Scale and detail

Size is a band: bounded below by the mark's own floor and above by its tier.
* **The floor** is set by the mark's finest critical element (descriptor lettering, counters, hairlines, inner gaps), not by the canvas proportion. Below the floor the variant changes (5.3); the mark is not shrunk past legibility.
* **The ceiling** is its tier. Surplus canvas goes to anchor scale and negative space, not to the mark by default.
* **Detail follows scale:** large marks can carry fine construction detail; small marks carry the simplified variant, coarser strokes, fewer parts, and open counters.

### 5.3 Representation

The master mark yields a ladder of variants that share its identity. Typical variants: the full lockup, a stacked or horizontal rearrangement, a compact variant with the descriptor dropped, a symbol-only or monogram form, and one-color and reversed forms (Facet I7). Choose the variant for each appearance from the role, available space and axis, and the ground. State, in the ladder, what is invariant (structure, silhouette family, lettering character, symbol, color logic) and what changes (arrangement, detail, descriptor, color count). A variant that drops an invariant is a different mark.

### 5.4 Placement, ground, and clear space

* **Placement** follows function, relationship to what it identifies or closes, and counterweight (`03`, Section 5.3). No canvas edge or end of the path is reserved for the mark.
* **Ground:** the mark keeps its separation on its ground by its boundary, its isolating clear space, or the contrast of its own color field. On busy grounds the mark takes a calm ground or its own field, not extra detail.
* **Clear space** scales with the mark's weight and role: a dominant mark is isolated generously, a signature keeps a modest, constant margin. The space is part of the mark's presence.
* **No overlap** with text or imagery. A mark that touches other elements loses fixity.

### 5.5 Relation to the message

The mark never competes unnecessarily with the main message. Weigh it in the weight audit with every other element (`03`, Section 5.1): where it outweighs its tier, reduce scale, contrast, or detail by moving down the ladder before changing its structure. Where it is the message (dominant identity), the rest of the hierarchy steps down from it.

### 5.6 Name appears once

The brand name string appears in the mark and is not re-set elsewhere as a headline unless the brief places it again. When the name is also the Tier 1 text, the mark is that element, and its construction (Section 3) carries both roles. The descriptor and tagline are separate strings, each once.

### 5.7 A family of pieces

When a brief covers several pieces, the master mark and its ladder are shared (Phases 1 and 2). Each piece derives its own role, variant, scale, and placement (Phase 3), so the mark is recognizably the same across pieces while its representation follows each medium.

## 6. Translation into the Prompt (Phase 4)

* **The mark is one entry**, in the mark slot, stated once (`07`, Section 8). It holds the mark's type and construction, silhouette, lettering traits and the moves that make it its own, the symbol as visible form, the boundary or open silhouette and the lettering's relation to it, the mark's own color logic and separation from the ground, internal levels and descriptor, the variant, and its relative scale and clear space in the hierarchy. The brand name appears here, in quotation marks, once.
* **Describe the construction, not the category.** "A wordmark in [typeface]" or "the logo" leaves the model to set text. State the unit: what contour it has, how the parts are held together, what is built into the lettering.
* **No rationale and no situation.** What the mark is for, where it hangs, and from how far it is read are analysis (Principle 12). Their visible consequences enter as scale ratios, clear space, contrast, and boundary behavior.
* **Directable by generators.** The mark is directed with a distinct, simple construction: one silhouette, few parts, flat fills, lettering by structure (`07`, Section 5). Supplied marks are reserved or placed, never recreated.

## 7. Verification & Diagnostics

| Symptom in a draft | Likely root cause | Revise at |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The mark reads as the headline | the mark was decided as typography; headline test failed | 1 (tests), 3.2 |
| A generic badge around a typeface name | boundary treated as the distinction; lettering not built | 3.3 (qualification) |
| Every mark in the batch is an open wordmark or every one is enclosed | construction defaulted; candidates did not differ in idea | 3.6 (diverge), 3.3 |
| The mark is the biggest thing though the offer is Tier 1 | role not derived from the hierarchy | 5.1, 5.5 |
| The mark vanishes on a busy ground | separation not designed; reduction ladder missing | 5.4, 5.3 |
| A small version loses the symbol or gaps fill in | floor ignored; variant not chosen | 5.2, 5.3 |
| The brand name appears twice | mark and headline both set the name | 5.6 |
| The prompt says "a logo" or "a wordmark" with only a typeface | construction not translated | 6 |
| The mark's structure changes between pieces | identity and representation not separated | 5.3, 5.7 |
| The mark copies a famous mark's construction | field grammar not examined for habits and clichés | 2.1, `05`, Section 3 |
