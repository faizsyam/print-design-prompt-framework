# Style Bank: Facets A to P (Vocabulary)

*Consulted in Phases 1 to 5 of `SKILL.md` according to `05`, Section 1. A vocabulary and a set of lenses for deriving specific design directions. It is not a menu, a rulebook, or a substitute for the analysis in Phase 1. Consultation procedure and guardrails: `05`. Phrasing for the prompt, generator reliability, and the specificity audit: `07`.*

Entries describe **characteristics** and what they tend to **signal**. They never say what suits a business: suitability is decided only by the drivers in the Decision Context (`SKILL.md`, Section 2) and the implication map (`01`, Section 4).

**Reading the tables.** Column names recur across facets with a fixed meaning.
* **Traits / Defining traits**: observable properties that separate the entry from its neighbors.
* **Vocabulary**: terms to reuse when phrasing a prompt, so a direction becomes visible and repeatable.
* **Signals**: what the entry tends to connote. A tendency, never a requirement, and always read through the audience's own codes.
* **Considerations**: limits, saturation, production tolerance, and what clashes. These are cautions for judgment, not prohibitions.

## Facet A. Lineages, Movements, Vernaculars & Sensibilities

Columns: **Direction** | **Defining traits and execution vocabulary** | **Tends to signal** | **Considerations**. Sections A5a and A5b use their own columns because commercial vernaculars and regional traditions need different information.

### A1. Rational and modernist

| Direction | Defining traits and execution vocabulary | Tends to signal | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| International Typographic (Swiss) | Mathematical grid; flush-left ragged-right neo-grotesque in few sizes; asymmetric balance; objective unretouched photography; generous space; rules as structure | Order, neutrality, clarity | Needs air; dense price-heavy banners lose its logic unless the information is truly gridded. Saturated in studios and clinics |
| Bauhaus and New Typography | Primary colors with black; bars, circles, diagonals; lowercase geometric sans; lettering locked to geometric forms | Constructed, progressive, didactic | Strong at poster scale; lowercase-only setting reduces recognition of names at distance |
| Constructivist | Diagonal axes, heavy bars, red-black-cream, photomontage, directional and rotated type | Urgency, mobilization, campaign | Easily reads as political; suits launches and events more than hospitality warmth |
| De Stijl | Orthogonal grid; flat primary planes; thick black dividers; asymmetric panel balance | Pure order | Rigid; excellent for panel-based menus or modular offers, poor for organic subjects |
| Isotype and pictogram systems | Standardized flat figures; repeated units counting quantity; silhouette clarity; one stroke and grid | Information for broad publics | Reliable at distance; needs a consistent icon set, which generators drift on |
| Polish poster school | One witty painted or collaged image; metaphor over depiction; hand lettering woven into the image; flat or brushy color | Cultured, idea-led | Requires a sharp idea; poor fit where the product itself must be shown plainly |
| Postwar corporate modernism | Modular marks in strict systems; restrained palette; neutral sans; consistent clear-space | Institutional competence | Cool and impersonal; useful for services seeking trust at scale |
| Scandinavian modernism | Soft geometry; natural-materials palette; humanist sans; quiet restraint; pale wood and textile tones | Calm, democratic, domestic | Easily drifts to category-generic café minimalism |
| Japanese modernist graphic | Asymmetric balance; bold scale shifts; vertical type; simple circular and fan forms; mass against void | Refined contrast | Works with strong single forms; empty space is the design, so copy must be short |
| Neo-grotesque minimalism (contemporary) | One neutral sans; tight tracking; monochrome plus one accent; ample space | Polished, neutral | Very saturated and easily generic; weak local identity |
| Op art and kinetic geometry | High-frequency black-and-white stripes, checks, concentric rings; optical vibration; moiré | Dizzying energy, graphic punch | Vibration hurts legibility; use as a bounded field, never behind text |

### A5a. Indonesian commercial and street vernaculars

These are the visual languages local audiences already read fluently. They carry strong credibility in their native placement and can be used faithfully, questioned, or refined, but the choice depends on the drivers.

| Vernacular | Defining traits and execution vocabulary | Signals | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Warung and kaki lima banner | Vinyl or fabric spanduk; saturated flat fields; oversized heavy lettering with outline and drop shadow; food photographs or painted dishes; stacked information; arrows and bursts | Immediacy, abundance, honesty | Strong read at a glance; the failure mode is equal emphasis everywhere. Refinement means imposing hierarchy while keeping the energy |
| Hand-painted papan nama and sign painting | Brush-built letters with shade and outline; flourishes; simplified painted objects (tire, scissors, teapot); color-blocked grounds | Local craft | Letter construction should show brush logic: stroke endings, even weight, shadow offset |
| Truck-rear and angkot art | Airbrushed or painted portraits; slogans; ornate frames; high saturation; chrome-like lettering and flames | Bravado, humor, folk wit | Humor register must match the brand; do not use as tokenistic ornament |
| Bus and AKAP livery and Pantura sticker art | Cutting-sticker lettering, stacked striping, gradient airbrush faux-chrome, destination name in bold italic | Roadside mobility, long-distance travel | Reads well on vehicles; cut vinyl cannot hold gradients |
| Hand-painted cinema billboard | Oversized painted faces; dramatic color; lettering integrated with figures; strong light direction | Spectacle | Requires convincing painted rendering; portraits are generator-fragile |
| Poster film Indonesia era (1970s to 1990s) | Painted or collaged key art; faces in layered collage; title in embossed or extruded lettering; bold red and yellow accents; cast names in small condensed capitals | Retro popular entertainment | Fits events, entertainment, and humorous retro tone |
| Cassette cover and pop-nostalgia ephemera | Studio portrait with airbrush glow, handwritten titles, color bars, boxed catalog numbers | Nostalgic youth culture | Low-detail nostalgia; useful for music and apparel |
| Komik Indonesia and cartoon strips | Bold ink outline; panel borders; speech balloons; onomatopoeia; limited flat color | Playful narrative | Mascot-driven brands; needs consistent character model |
| Kemasan jadul (vintage Indonesian packaging) | Mid-century offset; emblem-centered layout; ornate borders; few flat inks; naive illustration; Latin display type; slogan ribbons | Remembered everyday trust | Used widely in snacks and traditional products; saturated, so inflect it |
| "Cap" label vernacular | Product named "Cap" plus an emblem (animal, tool, plant, sun); circular or oval seal; heavy border; bilingual text; stacked claims | Established, patriarchal family trade | Works for kecap, jamu, minyak, kerupuk, rempah. Do not invent claims such as medicinal effects |
| Jamu and obat tradisional vernacular | Botanical engravings; figure of a woman with a gendongan basket (jamu gendong); herb illustrations; small-format sachet and bottle labels | Herbal tradition, remedy | Health claims are regulated; keep the claim visual to ingredients, not cures |

| Independence-era poster | Bold painted heroic figures; hand-brushed slogans; red-and-white emphasis; flat dramatic color | Collective resolve | Political and patriotic connotation; do not reproduce flag or emblem |

| Warteg and rumah makan signage | Green or blue-painted wood and glass cabinets; hand-painted name; menu words stacked on glass; floral curtains; white tile; lamp warmth | Affordable home cooking, everyday trust | Honest and dense; clean-ups must keep the abundance |

### A5b. Indonesian regional and cultural traditions

Use these as **structure and idea** when the brand's identity claims them. The Sensitivities column lists known cautions; it is not exhaustive. When a tradition's motif has a ceremonial or reserved status, derive from its geometry or palette logic instead of copying the motif.

| Tradition | Visual traits | Execution vocabulary | Sensitivities and use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Batik: Solo and Yogyakarta court (sogan) | Indigo, soga brown, cream; disciplined geometric repeats; rhythmic diagonals | Parang, kawung, truntum, sido mukti; hand-drawn wax (tulis); crackle; resist-white linework | Some patterns were historically reserved for royalty or ceremony (large parang forms, for example). Prefer palette and rhythm over motif |
| Batik: pesisir (Pekalongan, Lasem, Cirebon, Indramayu) | Bright and mixed colors; flowers, birds, butterflies; Chinese and Dutch influences; red ("abang getih pitik" in Lasem); cloud forms (mega mendung in Cirebon) | Multi-color dye layers; fine-line floral fields; cap (copper stamp) repeats | Good source of vivid palettes; Chinese and Peranakan influences must not be flattened into generic "oriental" |
| Batik: Priangan, Betawi, Madura, and others | Priangan softer pastel and cream grounds with flora and fauna; Betawi bold, bright with local objects; Madura strong red, black, and green contrasts | Palette logic by region | Check regional claim before naming |
| Tenun and ikat (Sumba, Flores, Timor, Lombok) | Deep indigo and morinda red-brown; horses, roosters, ancestral figures, hooks; feathered ikat edges; banded repeats | Warp ikat, weft ikat, supplementary-weft; stepped diamond; natural-dye palette | Some motifs are ancestral or rank-bearing; avoid placing figurative motifs on disposable items |
| Songket (Minang, Palembang, Malay, Lombok) | Gold or silver supplementary-thread geometry on dark ground; framed fields; repeating borders | Pucuk rebung (triangular bamboo-shoot edge), flower-and-bud repeats, metallic spot or foil | Gold implies ceremony and wealth; match price level |
| Ulos (Batak) | Red, black, white bands; woven stripes; stepped geometric ornaments; fringes | Banded stripe structure; stepped repeats | Types of ulos have specific ceremonial roles; use palette and banding as structure |
| Gorga and carved house ornament (Batak, Minang, Toraja, Jepara) | Dense scrolling or geometric relief; symmetry; painted accents in red, black, white, and gold | Relief carving; deep shadow; painted accent; spiral and tendril | Toraja and Batak carvings carry ancestral and funerary meaning; avoid casual ornament |
| Minang: rumah gadang and ukiran | Gonjong upswept roof ends; flowing vegetal carved panels; red, black, gold | Silhouette roof as emblem; ukiran-derived scrolls | Rumah makan Padang code is strongly claimed by that category |
| Betawi | Gigi balang (sawtooth eave trim); ondel-ondel (giant puppets with red or white faces); bright color, lenong theater | Triangular trim; folk-poster palette | Ondel-ondel is a cultural figure; use respectfully |
| Sunda | Wayang golek (three-dimensional rod puppets); angklung and bamboo; rice-field motifs; Sundanese script | Bamboo weaving texture; soft green palettes | Sundanese script requires accurate copy |
| Javanese courts and wayang kulit | Elongated profile figures; perforated filigree; gunungan (mountain-shaped leaf) as frame; warm backlit shadow | Cutout silhouettes; punched ornament; sogan palette | The gunungan and wayang figures carry sacred narrative; use as structure |
| Bali | Dense carved relief; Kamasan-style painted narrative; penjor (curved festival poles); poleng black-and-white check; tropical greens and gold | Fine linework, flat mineral colors, relief shadow | Barong, Rangda, deity forms, and poleng are sacred; avoid as decoration on commercial surfaces |
| Dayak (Kalimantan) | Scrolling aso (dragon-dog) forms; stylized hornbill; fern-fiddlehead spirals; beadwork; black, red, yellow, white | Beadwork grid; scroll and spiral | Motifs are clan-bound; use palette and spiral logic |
| Toraja | Tongkonan boat-shaped roofs; geometric carved panels; red, black, yellow, white | Boat-roof silhouette; geometric carved fields | Funerary associations; consult the claim |
| Bugis-Makassar | Lontara script curves; checked silk sarongs (lipa' sabbe) in bold plaids; phinisi ship sails | Plaid structure; script as ornament only with accurate copy | Script must be accurate |
| Malay (Riau, Kepulauan Riau, Pontianak, Palembang) | Pucuk rebung edges; carved lattice; Jawi lettering; yellow and green | Border triangles; lattice fields | Yellow carries royal associations in Malay courts |
| Aceh | Pinto Aceh (door) motifs; gold-thread embroidery; red, black, green, and gold | Ornamented door frame; embroidered border | Regional Islamic identity; handle with respect |
| Papua | Asmat-style carved forms; bird-of-paradise motifs; noken bag weave; earthy ocher, black, white | Carved relief; looped-bag pattern | Ancestor figures are sacred; avoid commodifying |
| Chinese-Indonesian festive and commercial | Red-and-gold; lanterns; zodiac animals; paper-cut windows; calligraphic couplets; coin motifs | Paper-cut (kirigami) pattern; gold foil on red | Characters must be real and accurate: generators garble them. Supply exact text or avoid |
| Islamic commercial (busana muslim, katering, umroh, toko buku) | Mosque dome and minaret silhouette; arabesque geometry; green, gold, and white; Naskh or Thuluth for names | Girih fields; calligraphic headline | See the sacred-text guardrail (`05`, Section 3). Crescent usage depends on the owner's preference |
| Peranakan (Straits Chinese) | Polychrome floral tile; beaded and embroidered textile motifs; pastel pink, green, and blue; porcelain ware | Tile-grid borders; kebaya-lace patterns | Hybrid; avoid mixing with Chinese festive vocabulary without a claim |

## Facet I. Mark Construction

Facet I is vocabulary for designing the master mark and its representations (`09`). It never says which construction suits a business: construction is decided across candidates against the drivers (`09`, Section 3.3). I1 and I2 give types and reproduction tolerance; I3 the levers that create distinction; I4 boundary constructions, open and enclosed alike; I5 the moves that turn a name into a mark; I6 symbol sources; I7 roles and representations.

### I1. Mark types and construction

| Dimension | Options | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mark type | Wordmark (name as lettering); lettermark and monogram (initials); pictorial mark (recognizable object); abstract mark (non-literal geometry); emblem and seal (text integrated within a contained badge); combination mark (symbol plus wordmark); mascot mark (character as identity) | Abstract marks need meaning from context |
| Lockup arrangements | Stacked, horizontal, circular, or tiered | Test narrow vertical formats |
| Boundary constructions | Explicit enclosure, implied boundary, or open construction with a defined silhouette (full vocabulary in I4) | Decided across candidates; no construction is the default |
| Construction logic | Monoline, geometric grid-built, hand-drawn, negative-space, overlapped, or modular | |
| Reduction behavior | How the mark simplifies to one color, small size, and distance (see I7) | Each mark needs a one-color form and a small-size variant |
| UMKM-specific forms | Founder portrait (Pak, Bu, Mas, Mbak, Bang), "Cap" emblem with an animal or object, name plus ordinal or place (Asli, Jaya, Sari, Barokah), mascot as owner | Portrait marks need a drawn, not photographic, rendering |

### I2. Reproduction methods and tolerance (rules of thumb; confirm with the printer)

| Method | Tolerance | Minimum detail guidance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sign painting | Moderate; shapes must be paintable | Thick strokes, no fine hairlines |
| Rubber stamp | Low to moderate | Strokes at least about 0.5 mm; small counters close |
| Embroidery (bordir) | Low | Strokes at least about 1 mm; text height at least about 5 mm |
| Foil stamping | Moderate | Strokes at least about 0.3 mm; large solid areas problematic |
| Neon (tube or LED flex) | Very low | Single-stroke forms; no tight inner counters |
| Vinyl cutting (cutting sticker) | Moderate | Strokes at least about 1.5 mm; no gradients |
| Sablon on fabric | Moderate | Strokes at least about 1 mm; avoid fine gradients |
| Laser engraving | Fine detail allowed | Depends on material |
| Offset and digital print | High | Fine detail allowed |

### I3. Distinction levers (what makes a mark more than styled text)

Columns: **Lever** | **How it creates distinction** | **Vocabulary** | **Watch for**.

| Lever | How it creates distinction | Vocabulary | Watch for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Silhouette | A definite outer contour that remains when the letters are blurred | Block, arch, tapered, circular, stepped, notched, asymmetric, tall, wide | A contour any stack of words would produce |
| Structure | A specific arrangement or interlock of parts | Stacked, tiered, interlocked, nested, baseline-shared, modular, radial | A template stack (name above descriptor) with no relation between parts |
| Lettering | Letterforms altered or built for the mark | Ligature, shared stroke, custom terminal, substituted letter, constructed counter, uniform stroke modulation, cut or inline | Legibility of the name; drift in generated lettering |
| Symbol | An idea of this brand made form | Pictorial, abstract, monogram, character, negative-space symbol | A symbol that is decoration beside plain lettering |
| Boundary | An enclosure or implied boundary with a specific form, proportion, and relation to the lettering | See I4 | A stock shape holding set type |
| Color and material identity | A color relationship or construction that belongs to the mark | Two-color lock, one-color knockout, layered or inline fill, flat enamel, cut, stamped, stitched | A palette chosen only by category convention |

### I4. Boundary constructions (explicit, implied, and open)

Columns: **Construction** | **Observable structure** | **Tends to signal** | **Considerations**. Signals are tendencies read through the audience's codes and the field's baseline, never requirements.

| Construction | Observable structure | Tends to signal | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Roundel and ring | Circular boundary, text on an arc or centered, optional inner ring | Seal, stamp, completeness | Arc text bends letters; long names shrink |
| Shield and crest | Pointed or curved heraldic form with divisions | Lineage, protection, authority | Strong heritage connotation; check against `05`, Section 3 |
| Arch and arched label | Rounded-top form, stepped or plain | Welcome, portal, craft | Lettering sits in a shaped field |
| Label, plaque, and tag | Rectangular or tag form with corners, notches, or holes | Product, retail, ownership | Easily generic; the relation to the lettering must be specific |
| Pill and rounded rectangle | Soft-cornered rectangle holding or underlying the name | Friendliness, modern commerce | Very common in digital-native fields |
| Ticket and stamp edge | Notched or perforated edge, scalloped seal | Offer, event, provenance | Edge detail needs a reduction path |
| Geometric polygon (hexagon, octagon, diamond) | Regular polygon boundary | Order, precision, structure | Symbol and lettering must fit its angles |
| Cartouche and oval | Elliptical or ornamented frame | Formality, tradition | Ornament adds weight |
| Ribbon and banner | Unfurled band holding the name, possibly crossing a form | Announcement, celebration | Text on curves; reading order |
| Window and inset | Cut-out panel in a colored block, name knocked out | Boldness, signage | Needs a solid field |
| Subject-derived container | A form from the subject's world holding or becoming the mark (a vessel, leaf, tool, roof, key) | Specificity, concept | Silhouette must stay simple; avoid pasted motif |
| Implied block | A color field, paired bars, bracket corners, or negative-space cut that defines the contour without an outline | Modern restraint, structure | The block must be constant across variants |
| Lettering as boundary | The letterforms themselves form the contour (stacked, justified, shaped) | Directness, voice | Contour must survive blur |
| Open construction with strong silhouette | No boundary; distinction from silhouette, structure, lettering, symbol | Openness, directness | Must pass the headline and swap tests (`09`, Section 1) |
| Symbol-led lockup | A symbol carries the unit and the name is locked to it | Recognition, flexibility | The symbol needs an idea; the lockup needs a relation, not a pair |

**Lettering-to-boundary relations** (any enclosure): lettering inside with margin; lettering aligned to the boundary's axes; lettering touching or breaking the boundary; lettering shaped by the boundary; boundary built from the lettering.

### I5. Name-to-mark moves (turning a name into a construction)

| Move | Result | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ligature and shared stroke | Adjacent letters joined into one form | Keep the name readable |
| Letter substitution | A letter replaced or inhabited by a symbol or counter-form | One substitution; legibility of surrounding letters |
| Negative-space insertion | A form revealed between or within letters | Needs a solid, simple field |
| Weight and scale contrast between words | A dominant word and a quieter one in a fixed ratio | A ratio any layout could produce is not a move |
| Stack to a block | Words stacked and justified to a defined contour | Contour must be specific |
| Baseline shift, arc, or cut | Letters moved off a straight baseline by rule | Curved text and generators |
| Monogram extraction | Initials built into a compact form | Legibility for a long name |
| Terminal and stroke customization | Terminals, joins, and counters designed as a set | Consistency across letters |
| Inline, outline, and offset construction | A secondary line construction around or within the letters | Reduction to one color |
| Enclosure-derived letterform | Letter shapes that follow the boundary's geometry | Boundary and letters designed together |
| Uniform modular construction | Letters built from one module or grid | Reads as constructed; limit to short names |

### I6. Symbol sources and abstraction

| Source | Derives from | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The name | its literal meaning, sound, initials, or a numeral or place in it | The first association is a hypothesis (`SKILL.md`, Principle 4) |
| The subject's world | materials, tools, processes, and states (Facet K) | Reduced to a single idea; not the product depicted |
| The place or maker | local landmarks, a founder, a craft lineage | Earned by the brand's identity (`02`, Section 1); heritage per `05`, Section 3 |
| The brand core | the behavior or relationship the brand stands for | Needs a form that can show it |
| Abstraction level | literal, simplified, geometric, or abstract | Abstract marks need context to gain meaning; literal marks risk the field's cliché |

Construction logics for the symbol and lettering (monoline, geometric grid-built, hand-drawn, negative-space, overlapped, modular) are listed in I1.

### I7. Roles and representations

| Item | Description | Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Role: dominant identity | The mark as focal anchor; fullest construction; most isolation | Weight from isolation and contrast before area |
| Role: supporting element | Moderate scale; the variant that holds at that scale | Keeps separation without competing with the lead |
| Role: discreet signature | Compact variant; unit and silhouette intact | Near its floor; coarse strokes, fewer parts |
| Full lockup | The master arrangement with all internal levels | The reference form |
| Stacked and horizontal rearrangements | The same parts in a different axis | Suited to canvas shape; proportions stay fixed |
| Compact variant | Descriptor and fine detail dropped | Retains structure and silhouette family |
| Symbol-only or monogram form | The symbol or initials alone | Only where the symbol carries recognition |
| One-color and reversed forms | Single ink; light-on-dark | Boundary and counters hold; no gradients |
| Reduction ladder | The ordered variants, each stating what stays and what simplifies | The invariants (structure, silhouette family, lettering character, symbol, color logic) never drop |

## Facet M. Production & Viewing Conditions

Sizes, distances, and tolerances here are rules of thumb gathered from sign and print practice. Confirm with the printer, and treat them as constraints that inform choices, never as templates.

### M1. Viewing distance and legibility

A common signage heuristic gives about 2.5 cm of letter height per 3 m of comfortable reading distance, and roughly 2.5 cm per 9 m as an absolute limit under good conditions. Speed, glare, and thin strokes demand larger letters.

| Scenario | Typical viewing distance and speed | Read time | Implications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Spanduk on a main road | 10 to 40 m from motorbikes and cars at 20 to 50 km/h | 1 to 3 seconds | Name, category, one hook; bold type; value contrast |
| Fascia signage across the street | 10 to 25 m | 3 to 5 seconds | Name and category large; details small |
| Pavement and parking approach | 3 to 10 m | 5 to 10 seconds | Offer, menu hint, and contact readable |
| Shop window | 1 to 4 m | 5 to 15 seconds | Prices and items readable; glass reflections |
| Counter and queue | 1 to 3 m | 10 to 30 seconds | Menu and price lists; table of items |
| Handheld flyer and menu | 30 to 50 cm | Minutes | Smaller type permitted; leave margins |
| Vehicle livery | Moving; 5 to 30 m | 1 to 3 seconds | Contact in large digits |
| Roll-up stand at an event | 2 to 5 m | 3 to 10 seconds | Top-third message |

### M2. Process constraints

| Process | Strengths | Constraints that shape the design |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Digital outdoor print on flexi (eco-solvent, solvent, or UV) | Full color; any size; fast | Gradients may band; fine hairlines weak; edges hemmed |
| Offset | Consistent color; economical at volume | Plates and setup; minimum runs |
| Digital short-run | Low volume; personalization | Stock choice and size limited |
| Sablon on paper or fabric | Opaque solid colors; strong blacks | Few colors; no fine gradients; registration |
| DTF and sublimation | Full color on fabric | Fabric limits; wash |
| Cutting sticker | Flat colors; long outdoor life | No photographs or gradients; weeding limits |
| Bordir (embroidery) | Premium feel | Thick strokes; limited colors |
| Hot stamping and foil | Metallic accent | Large solids can fail; fine lines need large size |
| Emboss and deboss | Tactile | Needs sizable shapes and thick stock |
| Spot UV | Contrast of gloss and matte | Needs matte base |
| Laser cut and engraving | Precise | Material burn tones |
| Hand painting | Unique | Not reproducible exactly |

### M3. Safe areas and finishing

| Item | Common practice | Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bleed | Often about 3 mm for print; more for large-format | Extend backgrounds |
| Safe margin | About 5 to 10 mm on print; wider on banners | Keep text away from edges |
| Banner hems and grommets (mata ayam) | Hems of about 3 to 5 cm; grommets at corners and about every 50 cm | Keep text clear of the hem and the grommet line |
| Banner pockets and wind slits | For poles | Avoid text in pocket zones |
| X-banner and roll-up base | Lower portion hidden or curled | Keep the bottom about 15 to 20 percent free |
| Fold lines | Brochures | Avoid text crossing folds |
| Window decal | Reverse and transparent | Plan white backing |
| Resolution | Large format often about 72 to 150 dpi at actual size; small print about 300 dpi | Viewing distance sets it |
| Fonts | Convert text to outlines for print files | |
| One-color fallback | A mark or layout should survive in one ink | Test |

### M4. Environment

| Condition | Effect | Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tropical sun and UV | Fade; heat on vinyl | UV-protected laminate or ink |

## Facet P. Image Roles, Making Families & Style Signatures

*Consulted with `08` (Phases 1 to 5). Facet P is vocabulary and calibration. It never says which role a business needs or which style suits it, and it never gives a role's finished treatment: treatments are derived by `08`, Section 4, from the role's coordinates and the style's signature.*

### P1. Role archetypes (calibration lenses, not templates)

Recurring role profiles, to check the inventory for completeness (`08`, Section 1) and to calibrate dial settings. Columns follow the coordinates in `08`, Section 2. "Tendencies" are typical dial directions (`08`, Section 4.1), always overridden by the drivers of the actual brief.

| Archetype | Primary job | Typical grade | Reading regime | Typical relations | Truth demand | Dial tendencies |

| Anchor image | Evoke or identify | Dominant | Gestalt, then scene | Leader; figure on ground, or integrated | Record or idea | Resolution as high as one clear form allows; tonal range and chroma at full; Edge high; Completeness whole or bold crop |
| Proof image | Prove | Supporting | Detail | Accompanies type; sibling of other proofs | Record | Resolution high; Specificity high; Mark visibility of the record itself; tonal range moderate |
| Series item | Organize and distinguish | Supporting | Scene | Series member; accompanies a list | Record or idea | Identical dials across items; Resolution set by the smallest; Edge crisp on a shared ground; Completeness whole |

### P2. Making families (where identity lives and how it behaves under modulation)

Use to place any style, listed or not, and to anticipate how it behaves as dials move (`08`, Sections 3 and 4). Columns: where identity lives; what survives when dials are pulled down; what scales up when the style leads; typical mismatch.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| F1. | F2. Translucent and wet media | transparency and layering; edge bloom and bleed; ground showing through as light; pigment granulation | washes widen, layers thin, values lighten; bleed and ground-light persist; hold one edge or granulation anchor | layered washes with controlled hard edges at the focal point; added brush detail | washing out below visibility; muddy overlays at high density; heavy opaque hosting under type |
| F3. | F4.
