# Imagery by Role & Style Modulation

*Used in Phases 1 to 5 of `SKILL.md` (Section 3 lists which sections each phase opens). It extends the pictorial commitment (`01`, Section 3), the visual approach (`02`, Section 2), and image placement (`03`, Section 5.4) from one pictorial subject to every image in the piece. Vocabulary lives in `06`, Facet P. Prompt phrasing lives in `07`, Section 7. Every decision here follows the Decision Protocol in `SKILL.md`, Section 2, and is entered in the ledger.*

Images do many jobs beyond leading the piece: they prove, explain, identify, organize, contextualize, set atmosphere, embellish, reassure, and give type a place to sit. A style that answers one job does not answer the others by being copied onto them. A piece whose images all receive the hero's treatment is one-dimensional (Principle 6), and a piece whose images share only a style name is incoherent. This module makes the **image instance** the unit of decision, gives each instance a role defined by explicit coordinates, holds one style identity across the piece, and derives how that identity is expressed in each role through a small set of dials. It is a derivation method, not a list of per-style or per-role instructions.

---

## 0. Working Principles

1. **The image instance is the unit.** Every place where an image does work (a hero, a thumbnail beside a list item, a field behind type, a motif on an edge, an explanatory drawing, a portrait that reassures) is a separate decision with its own brief. Layers named in `02`, Section 2.1 are expanded into image roles; none is inherited from another.
2. **Role is coordinates, not a label.** "Background" or "decoration" names a position. A role is the combination of function, grade, reading regime, relations, and truth demand (Section 2). Two images in the same position can need different treatments, and two in different positions the same.
3. **One identity, many registers.** The piece has one style identity (a signature, Section 3). A **register** is that signature expressed at the dial settings a role calls for. Roles change how much of each trait shows, never which style it is.
4. **Dials, not special cases.** Role-dependent execution is derived from coordinates through dials (Section 4), so the same method serves every style, medium, business, and role, including ones not listed in the bank.
5. **Identity transfers; it is not deleted.** When a role forces a trait down, the recognizable character moves to traits the role can afford. A register in which no anchor survives is a different style.
6. **The piece is the judge.** An image is right when the whole composition works: its weight fits the tier budget, its relations to type, siblings, and ground are designed, and the images read as one authored family.

### The chain

Every image directive traces through the same chain, and no link is skipped:

1. **Context to jobs.** Business, objective, audience, medium, and placement (Phase 1 drivers) determine which jobs images should do and at what prominence (Section 1).
2. **Jobs to identity.** The concept, field position, and subject truth determine one style signature that can host every admitted job (Section 3).
3. **Job to role profile.** Each image's function, grade, reading regime, relations, and truth demand are decided from its job and the composition (Section 2).
4. **Profile to register.** The drivers set the dials, the anchor test and transfer keep the identity, and the reach check closes the gap (Section 4).
5. **Register to relations.** Placement, scale, and the relations to type, siblings, and ground confirm or reopen the dials (Section 5; `03`, Section 5.4).
6. **Relations to prompt.** Each register becomes one entry of visible properties (Section 6; `07`, Section 7).
7. **Prompt to piece.** The family audit, weight audit, and range audit judge the images together (Section 5.5; `04`, Section 5).

A finding that reaches no image decision, or an image decision with no driver behind it, is unfinished analysis (`SKILL.md`, Principle 10).

## 1. Image Inventory & Economy (Phase 1)

Run after the implication map's drivers are stated and before Phase 2 choices. Pictorial commitment (`01`, Section 3) is made per instance, not once per piece.

### 1.1 The jobs images can do

| Job | Delivers | Typical risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Evoke | desire, appetite, aspiration, mood | the shared look of the category or of stock imagery |
| Prove | evidence that the subject, process, result, or scale is real | weak capture or mismatch with the placement's register |
| Explain | comprehension of parts, steps, structure, location | over-detail; labels the generator garbles |
| Identify | recognition of brand, category, or character | genericness; borrowing the field's mascot or icon |
| Orient and guide | direction of the eye or the body, sequence | arrows and gaze that point off the piece |
| Organize and distinguish | faster scanning of lists, variants, and groups | inconsistency between items; sameness that hides difference |
| Contextualize | the subject placed in use, setting, or company | clutter; people the generator renders unreliably |
| Set atmosphere | a felt world, tempo, or temperature under everything else | competing with content; busy fields |
| Embellish | richness, finish, era, craft | weight without meaning |
| Reassure | trust through people, process, or supplied credentials | looking staged or fabricated |
| Support type | a place for type to sit or a contrast for it to stand against | a flat scrim as default; focal subjects landing under text |

The list is a tendency, not a closed set. A role can serve two jobs; name the primary and the secondary.

### 1.2 Procedure

1. **Find candidate jobs.** From the objective, reader task, message inventory, content structure (lists, variants, steps, locations), subject model, category field, and the information the placement expects, list each job an image could do that another layer would do worse. Use Facet P1 afterward as a completeness check, never to choose.
2. **Admit per instance.** Admission follows `01`, Section 3: the image does work the other layers cannot, and the required content at its floor leaves room. For each candidate state what type, color, shape, mark, or ornament would deliver instead and why the image does it better, or drop it.
3. **Grade per instance.** Absent, ambient, supporting, or dominant, the lowest that still does the job. Ambient runs down to barely perceptible. The piece's pictorial ceiling (`01`) caps the sum; only one image is dominant, and only when it is the Tier 1 element.
4. **Set the image economy.** Weight is a budget (`01`, Section 3): every image spends it through area, value contrast, chroma, detail, and placement. Grounds and full-bleed fields spend by area alone, so an ambient image is cheap only when its dials are low (Section 4). The sum of image weight, with each image below the weight of any higher tier, must fit the reception balance. Surplus is not spent on images by default.
5. **Guard the content.** An image depicts only what the brief supplies or what is a visible property of the supplied subject. It adds no item, variant, price implication, claim, endorsement, person, certification, or place. Trust imagery (credentials, regulated marks, supplied portraits) follows `05`, Section 3 and `07`, Section 5. Series images exist only for supplied items.
6. **Open the image role ledger.** One entry per instance: job (primary, secondary), grade, subject and its source in the subject model, the driver behind it, and the information status of that driver.

Outputs: the ledger entries, the provisional ceiling, and the image economy. Phase 2 completes each entry's coordinates, kind of making, and register.

## 2. Role Coordinates (Phase 2)

Complete five coordinates for each ledger entry. The line they form is the **role profile**. Each is decided from drivers; none is read off the image's position.

| Coordinate | Values | Decided from |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Function** | the jobs in 1.1 (primary and secondary) | objective, message allocation, what the other layers already carry |
| **Grade** | absent, ambient, supporting, dominant | sufficiency, tier, image economy |
| **Reading regime** | *gestalt* (read as one shape from afar), *scene* (scanned while approaching), *detail* (examined by choice), *felt* (not read but perceived as tone, texture, or field) | the tier of the image's own content and the viewing conditions in which that content, not the piece, is perceived |
| **Relations** | to type: *hosts* it, *accompanies* it, *carries* it (type inside or on the image's shape), *wraps* it, or is *separate*. To siblings: *leader*, *series member*, *subordinate*. To ground: *is the ground*, *figure on it*, or *integrated with it*. To the mark: independent (`09`, Section 3.7) | composition strategy, tiers, content groups |
| **Truth demand** | *record* (must show the real), *idea* (stands for a notion), *field* (no subject truth; texture, pattern, tone) | subject truth, the lived USP, what the audience needs to believe |

Derived from the coordinates: **scale regime** (the image's size relative to the canvas, and the subject's size within the image), set by grade, reading regime, and tier per `03`, Sections 5.2 and 5.4.

**Kind of making** (`02`, Section 2.2) is chosen per role, with truth demand as the first test: record leans captured, idea leans drawn or constructed, field leans constructed or any texture-bearing making. Roles may differ in kind of making only when the shared properties in Section 3.4 bind them and the difference is justified by function.

## 3. Style Identity: Signature, Anchors, Reach (Phase 2)

### 3.1 Signature

A style's **signature** is the small set of properties that make it recognizable. Decompose any style (listed in the bank or not) into six anchor classes using the reference decomposition in `07`, Section 4:

| Anchor class | Asks |
| :--- | :--- |
| Making logic | how marks and forms are built (stroke, wash, cut, carve, pixel, rendered form, exposure) |
| Edge logic | how forms end and meet (hard, bled, torn, outlined, cast-shadowed, optically focused) |
| Color logic | palette structure, ink or pigment behavior, value structure |
| Light and space logic | flatness or depth, direction and character of light, how space is built |
| Simplification logic | what is kept and what is dropped, silhouette versus built-up detail |
| Surface logic | the ground, texture, grain, relief, or material the work appears on or in |

The **primary anchors** are the two to four classes that most separate this style from its neighbors (`06`, Facet O2). Facet P3 lists them for the bank's imagery styles; for any other style derive them the same way. The remaining classes are **elastic**: they flex with the role.

### 3.2 Family and reach

Every style belongs to a **making family** (Facet P2), which says where identity lives, how it behaves when dials fall or rise, and where it fails. A style's **reach** is the span of dial settings over which its primary anchors stay perceptible. Reach is a property of the style and the generator, not a prohibition: outside it the style needs support (a bound register, a different carrier anchor, a change in scale) or the role goes to a bound sibling register.

### 3.3 Choosing the signature

The signature is chosen once for the piece, at the stance level (`02`, Section 2.3, and Section 2.4), from the concept, field position, and subject truth. It must also be able to host the roles the ledger holds:

* For each admitted role, state the register the signature would take (Section 4). A role with no workable register is a conflict: add a bounded influence that governs one property for that role (`05`, Section 2, step 4; Facet O3), change the role's kind of making and bind it (3.4), or revise the signature through the conflict rule.
* Test the signature against the dominant role and the most demanding recessive role together (typically the hero and the field that hosts type). A signature that survives only at full intensity is narrow; one that survives only at low intensity is thin.

### 3.4 Binding across kinds of making

When roles use different kinds of making (a captured hero with a drawn motif, a rendered subject with flat icons), the images are bound by properties shared across all of them: palette logic and the hue family, edge character, the direction and temperature of light or its deliberate absence, surface and grain behavior, and the simplification vocabulary. One kind leads and sets the voice (`02`, Section 2.5). Unbound difference reads as collage, valid only when collage is the chosen movement.

## 4. Modulation Engine (Phases 2 and 3)

### 4.1 Expression dials

Eight dials express any style in any role. A setting is a position between the ends, stated per image.

| Dial | Low end | High end | Controls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resolution** | silhouette; a few large forms | fine detail across the subject | how much is resolved, and the size at which marks become detail |
| **Tonal range** | compressed, even, narrow value band | full value spread with deep darks and bright lights | contrast, light drama, and the weight of the image |
| **Chroma and palette breadth** | near-monochrome or two hues | wide, saturated gamut | color weight and distinctiveness |
| **Edge and separation** | dissolved; forms merge with the ground | crisp contour; the subject isolated on its ground | figure-ground strength |
| **Depth and focus** | flat, single plane, even focus | deep layered space with melted foreground or background | spatial immersion; where sharpness sits |
| **Completeness and crop** | fragment, tight crop, bleed, abstraction | whole subject with room around it | how much of the subject the frame holds |
| **Mark and surface visibility** | making invisible; smooth | making declared: line, brush, grain, ink, relief, fiber | craft presence and tactility (cf. Facet J, craft visibility) |
| **Specificity** | typified, generic form | particular, identifiable subject | recognition versus mood |

Intensity is not a ninth dial. Restraint lowers settings along these dials before it removes character (Principle 6).

### 4.2 Dial drivers

Each coordinate and condition pushes the dials in a direction. They combine; none is a rule for a named role.

| Driver | Effect on the dials |
| :--- | :--- |
| Reading regime: gestalt | Resolution low (one clear form); Edge high; Completeness whole or a bold crop; Specificity may be typified; marks coarse in proportion to the subject |
| Reading regime: scene | Resolution medium, concentrated at the focal area and relaxing outward; Depth moderate to deep; Specificity high at the focal point |
| Reading regime: detail | Resolution high; Mark visibility high; Specificity high; Edge refined |
| Reading regime: felt | Resolution low (forms below recognition are allowed); Edge dissolved; Tonal range compressed; Specificity low; texture present as surface, not as subject |
| Relation: hosts type | In the host region: Tonal range compressed to a band that leaves the type's own contrast intact; Resolution low; Edge dissolved (no contour or focal form crosses it); Chroma restrained or kept away from the type's hue; Mark visibility even; the focal content sits outside it. The region is composed in the image itself, not rescued afterward by a flat overlay |
| Relation: accompanies type | Edge crisp and separated from the text by space; the image's contrast and chroma peaks stay below those of the type's tier |
| Relation: carries type (type inside or on the image's shape) | Completeness and Edge set by the carrying shape; Resolution moderate; Specificity as the shape needs |
| Relation: is the ground | Completeness bleeds off the edges; Edge dissolves at the margins; Tonal range compressed overall, with regions designed for calm and for content; no focal subject at the position type needs |
| Relation: figure on a ground | Edge high; Completeness whole; Depth shallow or isolated; the ground holds the surround |
| Relation: series member | Every dial identical across siblings; only the subject changes; vantage, light direction, subject scale in frame, ground, and crop are shared. Resolution is set by the smallest or most distant sibling |
| Relation: subordinate to a leader | Tonal range, chroma, Edge, and Resolution each sit below the leader's, on the same logic |
| Grade: dominant | Dials at the top of the range the reading regime and relations allow; every primary anchor fully visible |
| Grade: supporting | Tonal range, chroma, and Edge one step below the leader; anchors visible |
| Grade: ambient | Tonal range and chroma pull toward the ground, Edge dissolves, Resolution low; primary anchors persist at reduced amplitude |
| Truth demand: record | Specificity high; the record's own optical or material behavior, not an imposed hand; the real extent and scale of the subject shown |
| Truth demand: idea | Specificity may be typified; Completeness may be stylized or cropped; Mark visibility high enough to declare authorship |
| Truth demand: field | Specificity low; Completeness as repeat or bleed with no focal subject; Edge as tile joins or dissolve |
| Image scale | Mark size tracks the image's own extent and the regime: a large image carries finer marks relative to its subject, a small image needs coarser marks, fewer elements, and higher Edge. A texture that is rich at one scale is noise or vanishes at another |
| Substrate and process fidelity (analysis input) | Limits fine texture, gradients, and tonal granularity; sets Mark visibility and Tonal range. Converted to the visible character of the making, never stated as a manufacturing fact |

**Priority when drivers conflict:** the driver that protects a higher-tier element wins (a host region serves its type first); then the driver that protects the image's own job; then reading regime; then truth demand; then grade. A conflict that no dial setting resolves goes to the conflict rule.

### 4.3 Procedure per image

1. Read the role profile (Section 2).
2. Apply the drivers to set a target range for each dial; narrow it by the priority rule.
3. **Anchor test.** Remove the style's name from the register as written. Could a practitioner still tell which style it is? If not, a primary anchor was dialed out.
4. **Transfer.** Restore identity through the transfer moves (4.4), not by raising the dial that the role needs low.
5. **Reach check.** If no setting satisfies both the role and the anchors: change handling (scale, crop, subject simplification, which anchor carries), hand the role to a bound sibling register, or revise upstream through the conflict rule. Never silently drop the style.
6. **Write the register:** the dial settings resolved in the signature's own vocabulary (`07`, Section 7), for this image only.

### 4.4 Transfer moves

When a dial must sit low, identity moves to the dials the role can afford:

| Dial held low | Identity carried by |
| :--- | :--- |
| Resolution | edge logic, palette logic, the style's simplification vocabulary, mark character at coarse scale |
| Tonal range | hue relationships, surface behavior, mark character |
| Chroma | value structure, edge and line behavior, surface |
| Edge | palette logic and the marks continuing inside the forms; softness of the style's own kind (bleed, grain, atmospheric fade) rather than a generic blur |
| Depth and focus | edge, palette, mark behavior; flatness expressed in the style's own flat logic |
| Completeness (fragment crop) | the fragment must contain a primary anchor; mark and surface carry the rest |
| Mark and surface visibility | palette logic, light logic, simplification, shape vocabulary |
| Specificity | silhouette character, palette, mark behavior |

A generic blur, fade, desaturation, or opacity reduction is a default, not a transfer: it lowers every dial at once and removes the signature.

## 5. Relations (Phase 3)

Images are placed as parts of a composition. After zoning, derive each relation the role profile names.

### 5.1 Image and type

* **Hosting.** The host region is a designed region of the image: one value band, low detail, dissolved edges, no focal form, placed where the type sits. Where the image cannot offer such a region, change its crop, subject position, or dial settings first; use a tonal field or shape behind the type only when it belongs to the movement's own devices (`03`, Section 3).
* **Accompanying.** Image and text each occupy their own region, related by alignment and proximity; neither crosses the other.
* **Carrying.** When type sits inside or on an image shape, the shape is built for the letterforms' legibility and the image fills it at moderate resolution with its focal content inside the type's counters and strokes.
* **Wrapping.** Type follows the image's contour at a consistent offset; the contour is crisp enough to wrap.
* **Separate.** The regions are different canvas areas; space between them exceeds spacing within either.
* **No overlap with text** except hosting and carrying, which are deliberate integrations (`03`, Section 5.3).

### 5.2 Image and image

* **Hierarchy among images follows tier.** One leader; supporting images step down in scale, contrast, and detail; ambient images step down further. Two images sit at the same prominence only as siblings.
* **Series consistency.** Siblings share every dial and every framing property; they differ by subject and by nothing else. Where sibling subjects differ in color, silhouette, or complexity, the shared rules absorb the difference (a common ground, a common crop, a common scale of subject in frame).
* **Differences by role are exactly the role differences.** If two images differ in anything not explained by their role profiles, the derivation was skipped.

### 5.3 Image and ground

Decide whether each image is a figure on the ground, integrated with it (shared palette, grain, and edge behavior so it grows from the surface), or is the ground. Integration is achieved by shared properties, not by transparency alone. Where an image meets the ground or the canvas edge, state the edge behavior (crisp, fading in the style's own manner, torn, bled off).

### 5.4 Image and mark

The mark stays structurally independent of every image (`09`, Section 3.7). No image carries the mark's recognition.

### 5.5 Audits

* **Image weight audit.** Extend the weight audit (`03`, Section 5.1) to every image region, including full-bleed fields, bands of texture, and decorative motifs: assess the weight produced by area, value contrast, chroma, detail, and position, and compare to tier order. Reduce the image's dials before reducing its area, then its area before removing it.
* **Family audit.** Cover the subjects and look at the registers alone: do they read as one style, and is each difference traceable to a role coordinate?
* **Range audit.** The images, together, pay off at each distance from which the piece is read (`02`, Section 1), without repeating one note at one volume.

## 6. Translation into the Prompt (Phase 4)

* **One entry per image role**, stated once. The entry holds: the subject (supplied subjects only), its complexity, its kind of making and signature traits, its register as dial settings resolved to visible terms (`07`, Section 7), its edge and crop behavior, and what binds it to the other images. Position and relative scale go to the composition slot; rendering goes to the visual elements slot; imagery that forms or sits within the ground goes to the background slot, each at its register.
* **Role words stay out; their visible consequences go in.** "Hero", "background", "decorative", "supporting", "to host the text" never appear. Write the calm region, its position, its value band, and its dissolved edges. Write the repeated framing properties of a series as one shared rule.
* **No narration of function or situation.** What an image is for, where it is meant to be seen, and how far away are analysis, not depiction (Principle 12).
* **Resolve each register through the signature's own vocabulary.** The same dial setting is phrased differently for different families (`07`, Section 7).

## 7. Diagnostics

| Symptom in a draft | Likely root cause | Revise at |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Every image has the same polish, detail, and contrast | roles were not profiled; the hero's treatment was copied | Section 2 coordinates, Section 4 drivers |
| An image behind type competes with it | host region not designed; dials left at the leader's settings | 4.2 (hosts type), 5.1 |
| A secondary image looks like a different style | identity anchors dialed out, or no binding between kinds of making | 4.3 anchor test, 3.4 |
| Ambient imagery turns into a gray, generic veil | dials lowered together (generic fade) instead of transferred | 4.4 |
| Series items differ in angle, light, scale, or ground | sibling rules not shared | 5.2 |
| Decorative imagery adds weight but no meaning | embellish job not admitted against the alternatives | 1.2 |
| An image carries content the brief did not supply | content guard skipped | 1.2, step 5 |
| The prompt says "background image" or "to support the text" | role language in the prompt | Section 6 |
| A style cannot play a needed role | reach not checked at signature choice | 3.3 |
