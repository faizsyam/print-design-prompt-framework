# Style Bank: Consultation Protocol & Guardrails

*Used in Phases 1 to 5 of `SKILL.md` (Section 3 says what is opened and when). This is the first of three style bank files. `05` (this file) says when and how to consult the bank and what it must never be used for. `06` holds the vocabulary (Facets A to P). `07` says how a chosen direction is turned into prompt language and how reliably a generator renders each element.*

The bank is a vocabulary and a set of lenses for deriving specific design directions. It is not a menu, a rulebook, or a substitute for the analysis in Phase 1.

Generators and designers alike drift toward the nearest, most common treatment. Named, specific design languages pull output toward real design, and vague adjectives pull it toward the average. The bank widens the candidates available and gives each one precise, visible meaning. Entries describe **characteristics** and what they tend to **signal**. They never say what suits a business: suitability is decided only by the drivers in the Decision Context (`SKILL.md`, Section 2) and the implication map (`01`, Section 4).

## 1. When to Consult

* **Phase 1**, only after the independent investigation has produced its own findings, and only as a completeness check, never to choose a style:
  * Facet K (domain worlds): does the subject model overlook materials, processes, or local specifics of this kind of business?
  * Facets A5a and A5b: which vernacular and regional codes does the category field speak, and is each sorted as a signal of belonging, a habit, or a cliché?
  * Facets L and M: the medium's idioms, viewing distances, safe areas, and process constraints that set floors and capacity.
  * Facet N: the information components and conventions that the category and placement expect.
  * Facet I1 and I4: the field's mark conventions, sorted into signals of belonging, habits, and clichés (`09`, Section 2.1).
  * Facet P1: the image jobs and role archetypes, against the inventory the investigation produced (`08`, Section 1).
  * An entry that the investigation did not support is not imported into the Decision Context.
* **Phase 2**, after the field position and concept exist and before the choices are made: widen the candidates for kind of making, stance, movement, form language, palette, typographic voice, ornament, and mark expression (Facets A to C, E to J, O). For the mark, open Facet I3 to I7 and design across candidates that differ in construction idea (`09`, Section 3). Once a style direction is a candidate, read its family, primary anchors, and reach (Facet P2, P3), and derive a register for each admitted image role (`08`, Sections 3 and 4).
* **Phase 3**: composition structure, negative-space idiom, information zoning, and the surface and finish of devices (Facets D, G, H, N); image relations to type, siblings, and ground (`08`, Section 5; Facet P1).
* **Phase 4**: phrase each decision in precise, observable terms (`07`, including Section 7 for image registers), and check it against the medium (Facets L, M).
* **Phase 5**: audit specificity (`07`, Section 6), image registers and diagnostics (`07`, Section 7; `08`, Section 7), and producibility (Facet M, `07`, Section 5).

Understanding leads and vocabulary follows. Consulting the bank before the drivers exist narrows the search to what is listed.

## 2. Consultation Procedure

1. **State the needed visual behaviors.** From the concept and implication map, say what the design must do visibly: what it must signal, endure, and let the reader do. State behaviors, not styles.
2. **Scan wide.** Read the facet index at the top of `06` and open the facets the decision needs. Collect candidates from at least three families, including one you would not reach first and one from outside the category's usual sources. Then go beyond the bank: cross a lineage with a technique, a material logic, or a context from the subject's own world to originate directions it does not list. These candidates feed the Diverge step of the Decision Protocol (`SKILL.md`, Section 2): the nearest candidate is the *apparent* depth, the idea underneath it is the *underlying* depth, and the one this brand's character would choose is the *personal* depth.
3. **Test each candidate against the drivers.**
   * *Signal fit:* what it connotes against what the brand must signal, read through this audience's codes and price level.
   * *Credibility:* whether this business, at its scale and means, can deliver it and whether it looks native in the placement.
   * *Field position:* whether it keeps a signal of belonging, questions a habit, or replaces a cliché.
   * *Distance and capacity:* whether it stays legible and uncrowded at the viewing distances (Facet M).
   * *Producibility:* what the substrate and process preserve and what a generator renders reliably (Facet M, `07`, Section 5).
   * *Role reach:* whether the style has a workable register for every image role the piece needs, from the dominant image to the most recessive (Facet P3, `08`, Section 3.3).
   * *Originality:* whether it is merely the nearest, apparent choice.

   Reject with a one-line reason tied to a driver.
4. **Compose.** One dominant lineage governs structure, and for imagery its signature (`08`, Section 3) is the identity every image keeps. At most two secondary influences each govern a single property (texture, palette, mood, lettering, or ornament). Add a brand-specific inflection so the result is this brand's variant and not the lineage's default (Facet O3, O4). Influences must share a compatible logic (how flat or deep, ordered or free, hand-made or machined, restrained or lavish; Facet O1); contradictory logic produces muddy results.
5. **Resolve** each chosen term into visible properties across the direction's anatomy (`07`, Section 1). Keep a term only if a driver calls for it and it changes something visible.
6. **Record** what was chosen and rejected in the ledger (the Decision Context), each with its driver.

## 3. Guardrails

* **Vocabulary, not rules.** No entry is required, forbidden, or mapped to a type of business. Any combination is open if its logic is compatible and each part earns its place.
* **Characteristics over names.** The traits carry the meaning, so state them. Name movements, eras, techniques, regional traditions, and materials. Never name living artists, brands, characters, or other protected sources as the style.
* **Heritage and cultural motifs.** Use them only where the brand's identity claims them (`02`, Section 1), as structure or idea and not as pasted decoration. Treat ceremonial, sacred, royal, or status-bearing patterns with respect and keep them out of ornament. The regional table (A5b) lists known sensitivities; absence from the table does not mean absence of sensitivity.
* **Sacred text and names.** Quranic verses, the name of God, and sacred symbols of any faith do not belong on disposable, floor-level, or food-contact surfaces, or in decorative filler. Calligraphy used as pure ornament should be generic letterform rhythm, not recognizable scripture, unless the brand's identity and the owner explicitly claim it.
* **State symbols and regulated marks.** The national flag and state emblem are legally protected in Indonesia and are not decoration; red and white may be used as a palette without reproducing them. Official trust marks (halal, food-safety registration, payment QR) are reproduced only as supplied and only when the business holds them (Facet N5).
* **Revivals import whole eras.** A period style brings its type, color, printing limits, and imagery. Take what the concept needs and leave the baggage that contradicts the brand's means or the placement.
* **Saturation.** A style used everywhere in a category is category habit. Weigh it by how many competitors already speak it.
* **Marks.** A mark is built from this brand's name, subject, and core, never from the construction of an existing brand's mark, and never from the national flag, state emblem, sacred text, or regulated marks. Heritage forms in a boundary or symbol enter as structure or idea (`09`, Section 3.5). Enclosure forms with strong heritage connotation (crest, shield, cartouche) are weighed against the brand's own claim to that lineage.
* **Dress-up versus structure.** A tradition applied as surface (a batik pattern behind a logo) is weaker and riskier than one that informs structure (its geometry, rhythm, palette logic, or making process).
* **The bank is not exhaustive.** A missing term is not an exclusion.

## 4. Precedence & Boundaries

The bank serves the framework and never overrides it.
* **Hard content, the output template, and the execution rules** (`SKILL.md`, Sections 4 and 5) outrank every bank entry. A bank entry never adds, removes, or alters hard content, and never adds an element the brief does not supply.
* **Bank terms in the prompt, bank apparatus out of it.** Names of lineages, techniques, materials, and typographic structures appear in the prompt, always with their distinguishing traits (`07`, Section 2). Facet letters, entry codes, column names, ledger notes, and the words "bank", "facet", or "consider" never appear in the prompt (`SKILL.md`, Section 5, rule 3).
* **Numbers are for judgment, not for the prompt.** Sizes, distances, millimeter tolerances, and ratios in Facets I, L, and M inform decisions. They appear in the prompt only when the brief supplies them (`SKILL.md`, Section 5, rule 6).
* **Situation facets inform and never speak.** Facets L and M, the substrate and process columns of Facets B, G, and I, and every entry that describes where a piece hangs, how it is produced, or how it is viewed are lenses for judging decisions. They never supply prompt wording. Only the visible consequence of a decision they shaped is written, and only as depiction or rendering (`SKILL.md`, Principle 12).
* **Facet P informs; it never prescribes.** Role archetypes, families, and signature rows are inputs to the derivation in `08`. No row says which role a business needs, which style it should use, or how a role is finally treated, and a style absent from the index is signed the same way (`08`, Section 3).
* **An unlisted need is not a blocked need.** Where no entry fits, derive the direction from the drivers and the subject's own world, and resolve it with the same precision.
